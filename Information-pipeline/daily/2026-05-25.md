## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI Codex can now control your Mac while the screen is locked

OpenAI shipped "Locked Use" for its Codex desktop agent: the agent can operate macOS applications while the screen is off and locked. Users send tasks from their phone; Codex uses Apple Accessibility APIs and Screen Recording to click through windows, type, and navigate menus — then automatically relocks the device the moment local input is detected. Each allowed app requires explicit user approval; Terminal, system admin prompts, and Codex itself are blocked from automation. The feature is unavailable in the EU, UK, and Switzerland at launch.

**Why it matters (PM):** The barrier to always-on autonomous desktop agents just dropped to "install a plugin and grant permissions." Long-running QA flows, batch document processing, and overnight automated testing no longer require a human to stay logged in — which means agentic task design for Mac-native workflows is now a production pattern, not a prototype. The granular per-app approval model is the guardrail design pattern to study before your own teams build anything equivalent.

## 💰 Business & Funding

### Anthropic closes $30B+ at $900B+ valuation — now the world's most valuable private AI company

Anthropic is closing a funding round exceeding $30 billion at a pre-money valuation above $900 billion, expected to complete the week of May 26. Four firms co-lead with approximately $2B each: Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Altimeter Capital, and Greenoaks Capital Partners; Founders Fund and General Catalyst are also participating. The round vaults Anthropic past OpenAI's March-closing $852B valuation. The revenue backdrop is striking: Anthropic generated $4.8B in Q1 2026 and is tracking toward $10.9B in Q2 alone — growth driven primarily by Claude Code adoption in enterprise.

**Why it matters (PM):** Anthropic's valuation has grown roughly 15× in 14 months. That velocity is not a general "AI hype" signal — it specifically reflects Claude Code's demonstrated enterprise scaling. The capital gives Anthropic room to compete aggressively on pricing and enterprise terms. If you're building on Anthropic products, or competing against them, assume the next 12 months will be measurably more intense than the previous 24 combined.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** With $10.9B quarterly revenue and fresh capital, Anthropic can absorb loss-leader pricing on mid-market deals. The KPMG alliance (276K staff) and Intuit partnership (see Quick Hits) both closed this week — vertical market penetration is clearly where this capital is heading. Cost curves for Claude-powered features are likely to keep dropping.

### OpenAI files confidential S-1 with the SEC — IPO targets Q4 2026

OpenAI confidentially filed its IPO registration statement with the SEC on Friday 22 May, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters. The target listing window is Q4 2026, possibly as early as September, with some analysts pricing the debut at or near $1 trillion. Internal signals are cautious: CEO Sam Altman told staff "filing is different from being ready to go public," and CFO Sarah Friar confirmed retail investors will receive a share allocation. The financial picture disclosed internally: OpenAI is currently losing $1.22 for every $1 of revenue earned, against approximately $25B annualized run rate.

**Why it matters (PM):** The S-1 will be the first public, audited view of a frontier AI company's unit economics — loss ratios, compute cost structure, revenue mix, and growth rates. Those numbers will immediately become reference data in board conversations, vendor negotiations, and competitor analysis decks across the industry. Read the prospectus when it drops; it is the most useful single document for understanding the AI market structure of 2026.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Pope Leo XIV releases AI encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas* — first official Vatican doctrine on AI

Pope Leo XIV published *Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence* today, the first papal encyclical on AI. The 83-page document, released exactly 135 years after Pope Leo XIII's landmark workers' rights encyclical *Rerum Novarum*, warns that AI risks widening inequality, weakening democracy, and eroding human dignity. Anthropic co-founder and interpretability researcher Christopher Olah was invited to co-present at the Vatican press conference. Olah acknowledged that frontier AI labs "operate inside incentives and constraints that can conflict with doing the right thing," and framed the Church's independence from commercial and geopolitical pressures as a necessary counterweight in the governance conversation. The encyclical calls for international frameworks with explicit protections for developing nations.

**Why it matters (PM):** A sitting Pope and a frontier AI lab co-founder on the same stage is a governance-landscape event, not a cultural curiosity. The encyclical's framing — AI as a structural threat to democracy and equity requiring international coordination — is likely to accelerate regulatory discussions in Catholic-majority markets across Latin America, Southern Europe, and Sub-Saharan Africa. If your product ships AI features into those markets, the encyclical's language will shape the questions you get from local partners, regulators, and procurement teams.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Enterprise sales into Catholic institutions — universities, hospitals, NGOs, and the vast ecosystem of Church-affiliated organisations — will increasingly involve ethics-screening questions shaped by this document. Having prepared answers to "how does your AI align with *Magnifica Humanitas*" is a new procurement reality for a significant vertical.

### Project Glasswing: Claude Mythos found 10,000 critical vulnerabilities in one month

Anthropic published the first substantive results from Project Glasswing on May 22. Claude Mythos Preview — a restricted model Anthropic built for vulnerability discovery — has, through approximately 50 trusted partner organisations (Apple, Microsoft, Google, Cloudflare, Mozilla among them), collectively identified more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities in a single month. Cloudflare found 2,000 vulnerabilities including 400 critical-severity. Mozilla found and fixed 271 vulnerabilities in Firefox 150 — more than ten times the number identified during testing of Firefox 148 with Claude Opus 4.6. The biggest revealed bottleneck: some open-source maintainers have asked Anthropic to slow down disclosures because *patching velocity*, not *discovery velocity*, is now the binding constraint.

**Why it matters (PM):** Vulnerability discovery is no longer the hard part of software security — disclosure processing and patch deployment are. If your organisation ships software to enterprise customers (especially in regulated sectors), the pressure on your security response SLAs from AI-accelerated bug discovery will intensify sharply, independent of whether your team ever gets access to Mythos. Accelerating your CVE triage and patch-deployment pipelines is not a future consideration.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Chinese open-weights models now handle >45% of OpenRouter traffic — cost gap with US models is widening

OpenRouter usage data from Q2 2026 shows Chinese-developed open-weights models collectively processing more than 45% of all platform token volume, up from ~1.2% in October 2024. Kimi K2.6 (Moonshot AI), DeepSeek V3.2, Qwen 3.6 Plus (Alibaba), and MiMo-V2-Pro (Xiaomi) are the leading models. DeepSeek V3.2 benchmarks at approximately 90% of GPT-5.4 quality at roughly 1/50th the cost per token. Kimi K2.6 scores 87/100 on coding benchmarks — a 10-point gap to Claude Opus, at 3.6× lower cost. The growth trajectory suggests cost-efficiency, not capability parity, is the primary driver of adoption.

**Why it matters (PM):** If you are evaluating models for cost-sensitive features — especially coding agents, document processing, or high-volume classification — the Chinese open-weights tier is now a legitimate production option, not just a benchmark curiosity. The cost gap with US proprietary models is widening faster than the capability gap is closing. Ignoring this bracket in model selection is leaving significant margin on the table, and your engineering team is probably already aware of it.
