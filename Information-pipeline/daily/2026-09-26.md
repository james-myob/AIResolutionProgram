## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### OpenAI and Anthropic both ship next-generation flagship models within days of each other
OpenAI released its GPT-6 family (Sol and Luna) at meaningfully lower per-token prices than GPT-5.6, while Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5.5, which independent benchmark tracker Vals AI ranked #1 on its index at 69.7% accuracy, alongside a price cut versus the prior Opus generation. **Why it matters (mid-market):** Cheaper frontier tokens narrow the gap between what a well-funded competitor and a lean mid-market team can afford to build with — a model tier that was a cost-prohibitive stretch a month ago is now within reach.

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Anthropic runs 950 Claude agents for 21 hours and surfaces a previously unknown enzyme system
Anthropic's life-sciences research group set 950 Claude agents loose on a large DNA-sequence database over 21 hours, narrowing more than 200,000 candidate reverse transcriptases down to 20 and flagging an unusual repeating DNA pattern. Human lab testing then confirmed a new biological system Anthropic calls "array-associated reverse transcriptases" (found in bacteriophages); some outside scientists quoted by Bloomberg cautioned the significance may be somewhat oversold.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is a rare public "how many agents, how long, what result" account of a real research workflow rather than a demo — a concrete reference point next time someone asks what "throw more agents at it" actually looks like in practice, caveats included.
**Sources:** [Anthropic News](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system) · [The Neuron Daily](https://www.theneurondaily.com/p/what-950-claude-agents-found)

### Anthropic launches Claude Marketplace, bundling 2,000+ partner connectors and consulting partners
Anthropic introduced Claude Marketplace, a single destination combining 2,000+ third-party connectors and plugins (Atlassian, Google, Microsoft, and Salesforce among them), Claude-powered software from partners including CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Legora, Lovable, and Snowflake, and consulting partners Accenture and BCG. ## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Federal appeals court upholds Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation of Anthropic
In a 2-1 ruling, the D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals upheld the Department of Defense's "supply chain risk" designation of Anthropic. The designation traces back to a collapsed $200M Pentagon contract after Anthropic declined to grant the DoD unrestricted military access to Claude models; the ruling contradicts an earlier, separate district court decision that had gone in Anthropic's favor, and Anthropic is reportedly weighing a further appeal.
**Why it matters (PM):** A lab's model-access red lines just had a real, litigated cost attached to them — if you sell into government or defense-adjacent customers on the back of a frontier lab's API, this is a live example of "we won't grant unrestricted access" turning into a formal procurement barrier rather than just a policy statement.
**Sources:** [CNBC (via Hacker News)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/pentagon-anthropic-ai-risk-appeals-court.html)

## 📒 New Concepts

### Decision Models ("System One" models)
**Plain English:** A model class, typified by TypeSafe AI's "Jev," that doesn't generate text at all — it takes text in and returns only a calibrated numeric or probabilistic output (a confidence score or distribution), priced on input tokens only. The pitch: most business logic doesn't need an essay back, it needs a fast, cheap, well-calibrated number — a narrower, more classifier-like counterpart to general-purpose chat models, name-checking Kahneman's "System 1" fast-thinking framing.
**Origin:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/21/jev/) — picked up this week in two Hacker News front-page posts referencing "Jev-style decision models."
