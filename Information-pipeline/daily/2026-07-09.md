## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 (Sol, Terra, Luna) to general availability
OpenAI moved its GPT-5.6 family — Sol (flagship, complex coding and reasoning), Terra (balanced, high-volume business tasks), and Luna (fast, low-cost routine work) — from limited preview to public availability on 9 July, priced at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 per million input/output tokens respectively. Simon Willison's early testing found Sol "definitely very competent" but not a clear upgrade over Claude Fable 5 on the coding tasks he regularly runs, while OpenAI's own benchmarks show Sol ahead on long-horizon agentic tasks (Agents' Last Exam) but behind on SWE-Bench Pro.

**Why it matters (PM):** Don't take the headline benchmark at face value — Sol wins on agentic autonomy, loses on the coding benchmark most teams actually anchor cost decisions to, so re-test against your own workload before switching.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Luna's $1/$6 pricing is aggressive enough to make agentic features viable for teams that couldn't justify frontier-model costs six months ago — worth revisiting any build-vs-buy call you shelved on cost grounds.
**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/) · [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/9/gpt-5-6/) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-08/openai-to-roll-out-top-ai-model-globally-after-limited-preview) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/08/gpt-sol-ultra-openai-anthropic-grok)

### xAI launches Grok 4.5, trained jointly with Cursor
xAI released Grok 4.5, which Elon Musk described as "an Opus-class model, but faster, more token-efficient and lower cost." It's the first model trained jointly with Cursor on real developer session data, following SpaceX's roughly $60B acquisition of Cursor in mid-June. EU availability is delayed to mid-July pending regulatory clearance.
**Why it matters (PM):** A model trained on live developer telemetry (not just public code) is a different data moat than anything else on the market right now — worth watching whether Cursor's other integration partners get squeezed as a result.
**Sources:** [xAI](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-5) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/08/spacexai-grok-new-model) · [ExplainX](https://explainx.ai/blog/grok-4-5-public-launch-spacexai-july-2026)

## 💰 Business & Funding

### TeraWulf signs $19B, 20-year data-center lease with Anthropic
Bitcoin miner TeraWulf will build a 401 MW AI infrastructure campus for Anthropic at its Justified Data site in Hawesville, Kentucky, under a 20-year lease expected to generate ~$19B in contracted revenue. Initial capacity goes live in H2 2027, ramping to full capacity by early 2028; TeraWulf is simultaneously selling its stake in a separate Texas JV to Fluidstack.
**Why it matters (PM):** Frontier labs are now locking in compute supply a decade-plus out via non-traditional landlords (crypto miners repurposing sites) — a sign that compute scarcity, not model access, is the binding constraint labs are actually planning around.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Illinois signs nation's first mandatory frontier-AI safety-audit law
Governor Pritzker signed the AI Safety Measures Act, requiring "large frontier developers" (>$500M annual revenue) to publish safety plans addressing catastrophic risk, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours (24 hours if imminent), and undergo annual independent third-party audits — a first-in-the-nation requirement. The law takes effect 1 January 2028 and was supported by Anthropic during its passage.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is the first state law with recurring third-party audits rather than a one-time check — expect it to become the template other states copy, so if your product embeds a frontier model, ask your vendor now whether they're already building toward this bar.
**Sources:** [Governor Pritzker's Office](https://gov-pritzker-newsroom.prezly.com/gov-pritzker-signs-nation-leading-artificial-intelligence-safety-law) · [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/politics/2026/07/06/ai-regulations-illinois-law-pritzker-signed) · [Transparency Coalition](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/illinois-gov-pritzker-signs-landmark-ai-safety-measures-act-into-law)
**Tags:** `entity:illinois` `entity:anthropic` `theme:safety` `theme:regulation`

### EU finalises AI Act "Digital Omnibus" — high-risk obligations deferred 16 months
The Council of the EU gave final sign-off to the AI Act simplification package on 29 June, formally adopting it ahead of the 2 August deadline. Annex III high-risk-use obligations move from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027; separately, the package adds a new prohibition on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM, with a shortened 3-month transparency-compliance window (new deadline 2 December 2026).
**Why it matters (PM):** The compliance clock for most high-risk AI use cases just moved out by well over a year — if you'd built a 2026 EU compliance deadline into your roadmap, you likely have real runway back, but the deepfake/nudify ban is live on a much shorter clock and applies regardless of company size.
**Sources:** [Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/29/artificial-intelligence-council-gives-final-green-light-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/) · [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-act-update-eu-resolves-to-change-rules-and-extend-deadlines) · [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Gemini 3.5 Pro slips to 17 July as DeepMind loses Shazeer and Jumper; Alphabet loses $225B
Google delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro from its original window to 17 July after scrapping the existing Gemini 2.5 Pro architecture for a ground-up rebuild targeting math reasoning and a 2M-token context window. The delay coincided with four senior DeepMind researchers — including Transformer co-inventor Noam Shazeer and Nobel laureate John Jumper — departing for OpenAI and Anthropic, a combination that wiped roughly $225B off Alphabet's market cap.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is a rare case where a lab's internal talent losses became directly visible in a public shipping delay and stock move — worth treating "who's leaving which lab" as a leading indicator for roadmap risk on any product you've built around a specific frontier model's cadence.
**Sources:** [The Agent Report](https://the-agent-report.com/2026/07/google-gemini-3-5-pro-delayed-july-2026/) · [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/6f0c6bb2-795f-4c57-9d09-6db691d7638a) · [HeyGoTrade](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/google-io-2026-gemini-deepmind-contextual-ai/)

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### Ethan Mollick: "the twilight of the chatbots" — experts now manage agents instead of prompting them
Mollick argues the field is shifting from non-experts using chatbots to fill knowledge gaps toward experts directing agents that do sustained, multi-step work unsupervised. His headline example: Claude Opus 4.7 ran unattended for 14 hours and built a software package he estimates would take a human engineer 2-17 weeks, for about $251 in tokens. He also notes a quarter of surveyed OpenAI staff already run four or more agents concurrently, and adoption is spreading evenly into legal and HR, not just engineering.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product's mental model is still "user types a prompt, gets a reply," you're behind the usage pattern Mollick is describing — the more useful design question now is what a user delegates and checks in on, not what they type.
**Sources:** [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-twilight-of-the-chatbots) · [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ethan-mollick-agents-are-replacing-chatbots-for-real-work)
