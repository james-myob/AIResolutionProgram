## 🔬 Research & Papers

### OpenAI reasoning model disproves the Erdős unit-distance conjecture

An unnamed general-purpose reasoning model from OpenAI has disproved the planar unit-distance problem — a geometry conjecture Paul Erdős posed in 1946 and mathematicians believed was approaching from the direction of square grids. The model discovered an infinite family of constructions that beats every previous bound, with the insight bridging algebraic number theory and discrete geometry in a way human researchers had not connected. The resulting 125-page proof was independently verified by mathematicians Noga Alon, Melanie Wood, and Thomas Bloom.

**Why it matters (PM):** The signal is not the maths — it's the method: a *general-purpose* model, not a specialist maths system, produced an original cross-domain discovery. If this pattern generalises to biology, materials science, or drug design, "AI accelerates research" stops being a marketing claim and starts being a procurement decision. Worth one briefing mention to your research or data-science stakeholders.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Anthropic ships two new agentic patterns: Dreaming and Outcomes

Anthropic's Code with Claude London event (May 19–21) is wrapping today after a week of live developer showcases. The two features that most change how teams should think about production agents: **Dreaming** — a scheduled background process that runs between active sessions, reviewing past task logs, extracting patterns, and writing memory notes the agent uses on its next run, without human prompting; and **Outcomes** — users write a rubric defining what "good" looks like, a separate grading agent scores every output against it, and automatically kicks back anything that fails for another run. Anthropic measured the grading-loop approach at 8.4% quality improvement on Word documents and 10.1% on PowerPoint decks with no model change.

**Why it matters (PM):** The "Outcomes" number matters more than it sounds. A 10% quality uplift from adding a review loop — with no model change — means most teams are currently leaving quality on the table not because their model is bad, but because they have no systematic way to check agent outputs before delivery. Map your current agent QA process against this pattern: is evaluation built into the loop, or happening after the fact?

**Why it matters (mid-market):** For mid-market teams buying Claude Cowork or building on the Claude Platform, "Outcomes" gives a concrete, quantified QA framework to put in front of a compliance or legal buyer. The grading-loop story ("the AI checks itself before it sends") addresses the single most common mid-market procurement objection to agentic workflows.

### Meta begins 8,000 layoffs the same week it posts record $56B quarterly revenue

Meta started notifying approximately 8,000 employees (10% of its ~79,000 workforce) beginning May 20, with further cuts expected in August and the autumn. The rationale: redirect payroll into GPU purchases and data centres against a projected $145B AI capital expenditure budget for 2026. The context: Meta simultaneously reported Q1 revenue of $56.31B and net income of $26.8B — its best ever quarter.

**Why it matters (PM):** The pairing of record profits and mass layoffs is the clearest single data point yet on what AI-driven restructuring looks like in practice at Big Tech scale. The bet is explicit: fewer human roles, more autonomous AI work. Whether this bet delivers at scale — not in demos — is the most important performance indicator to watch over the next 18 months for anyone building AI-augmented product teams.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** When a company with $26B net income cuts 10% of its workforce for AI investment, the board-level calculus has visibly shifted. Mid-market leaders should expect the same calculus to surface in their own board conversations over the next 2–4 quarters; having a prepared answer to "where are we in this trade-off?" is now table stakes.

## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Cohere open-sources Command A+ under Apache 2.0 — first enterprise-grade fully open model

Cohere released Command A+, a 218B-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model where only 25B parameters are active at any inference step, under a full Apache 2.0 licence. The model runs on a single NVIDIA Blackwell B200 GPU or two H100s, supports lossless 4-bit quantisation (W4A4), includes native citation generation (grounding claims to source documents via explicit tags), and covers 48 languages. Cohere is positioning this directly at "sovereign AI" use cases: regulated industries and government deployments that require full control over their inference stack.

**Why it matters (PM):** The hardware headline ("runs on two H100s") is the one to quote to enterprise stakeholders. For compliance-constrained teams that have been told "open-source is not production-ready", Command A+ closes that objection: it's Apache 2.0, enterprise-grade architecture, and deployable on hardware the organisation already owns.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-market teams in healthcare, legal, finance, or government contracting that have been blocked on SaaS AI due to data-residency requirements now have a viable, fully open option backed by verifiable citations. Expect this to unlock a class of mid-market deals that previously stalled at procurement.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Trump pulls AI executive order hours before signing: "I didn't like certain aspects"

A White House ceremony scheduled for Thursday afternoon to sign an executive order on AI and cybersecurity was cancelled by Trump personally. The order would have created a voluntary 90-day framework under which AI companies share new models with the government — and with critical infrastructure operators like banks — before public release. Trump told reporters: "We're leading China, we're leading everybody, and I don't want to do anything that gets in the way of that lead." The order has reportedly been stalled by internal disagreements multiple times.

**Why it matters (PM):** The practical consequence is continued US federal governance ambiguity. The vacuum is being filled at the state level: Colorado's comprehensive AI law for high-risk systems takes effect June 30. With no preemptive federal standard, state-by-state compliance requirements are the operative planning horizon for any product team serving US customers.

MIT TR's on-the-ground coverage of Code with Claude London (published today) highlights a specific dissonance: nearly half of attending developers had shipped pull requests entirely written by Claude in the past week — most without reading the code. ## 📒 New Concepts

### Agent Dreaming
**Plain English:** A scheduled, background memory-consolidation process that runs between an AI agent's active work sessions — while the agent is idle, a separate process reviews past task logs, extracts recurring patterns, surfaces lessons from previous errors, and writes consolidated memory notes. When the agent picks up a task next time, it wakes up with that context already integrated rather than starting fresh. The name is an explicit analogy to how human brains consolidate memory during sleep. The functional difference from standard RAG-based memory: dreaming is *proactive and periodic* (runs on a schedule, no trigger needed), not *reactive and on-demand* (retrieves when asked). Anthropic's implementation is the first to productise this pattern with a specific scheduled mechanism rather than leaving it implicit in long-context storage.
**Origin:** Anthropic Code with Claude 2026 — [event page](https://claude.com/code-with-claude) · [MindStudio breakdown](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/code-with-claude-2026-new-agent-features)
