## 🔬 Research & Papers

### OpenAI shares pretraining perplexity data and proposes it as a cross-lab metric
Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI's chief scientist, published the company's internal log-perplexity (how surprised a model is by held-out text; lower is better) figures: about 0.99 for GPT-3.5, about 0.91 for GPT-4, and below 0.8 for later models, which he links to emerging cyber and maths abilities. He expects further step changes around 0.68–0.70 and says AI-driven pretraining breakthroughs could come as soon as 2027, while conceding the metric is hard to compare across labs and ignores gains from chain-of-thought reasoning. These are OpenAI's own numbers and predictions.
**Why it matters (PM):** A lab is now publicly tying capability jumps to a single trainable number; treat the 2027 timeline as a lab claim, but plan roadmaps assuming continued step changes rather than plateaus.
**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### ChatPRD's Claire Vo: "token fraud" is overwhelming free trials
Claire Vo, who runs ChatPRD, describes people looping free trials and promo codes to get free AI usage and resell it, including a group that funds a Roblox/Fortnite map studio this way, and says she is ending free trials entirely. Simon Willison linked the talk; it is one operator's account, not a market-wide measurement.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your AI feature has a free tier or trial, model abuse and token resale as a cost line and add rate limits, verification, or card-on-file before launch.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-market products often bundle AI into existing seats; metering and fraud controls are cheaper to design in than to retrofit after a bill spike.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/token-fraud-is-here-and-its-bad/)
