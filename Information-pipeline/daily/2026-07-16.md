## 📦 Products & Tooling

### xAI open-sources Grok Build after a privacy backlash over silent directory uploads
Users discovered that xAI's Grok Build CLI agent was uploading entire local directories — in one reported case including SSH keys and password databases — to xAI's Google Cloud storage without clear consent. xAI disabled the upload behavior, committed to deleting retained data, and on 15 July released the full Grok Build codebase (~845,000 lines of Rust) under an Apache 2.0 license.
**Why it matters (PM):** Before approving any agentic CLI tool for your team, check what it captures by default and whether that's opt-in — "productivity tool quietly exfiltrates your filesystem" just moved from hypothetical to recently-realized failure mode.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/grok-build/)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### EU AI Act's chatbot-disclosure rule is now live and enforceable
The EU's Digital Omnibus package, finalized 9 July, pushed back several AI Act deadlines (high-risk use-case obligations now move to December 2027), but one requirement took effect immediately on 10 July: any business deploying a chatbot, virtual assistant, or automated conversational system to EU users must now clearly disclose that the user is interacting with AI. Penalties reach up to €35M or 7% of global annual turnover.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product has any conversational AI surface reachable by EU users, check today whether it discloses AI involvement clearly enough to survive a challenge — this is enforceable now, not a future deadline to plan around.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Smaller vendors without a dedicated compliance function are more exposed here than enterprises with legal teams already tracking the Digital Omnibus — a clear disclosure banner is cheap insurance against a very expensive fine.
**Sources:** [Teach AI Tools](https://teachaitools.blog/blog/eu-ai-act-enforcement-begins-july-2026) · [Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions-2/)
**Tags:** `entity:eu-ai-act` `theme:regulation` `theme:compliance` `lens:mid-market`

### Anthropic and OpenAI publicly diverge on state vs. federal AI regulation
Anthropic became the first major lab to endorse a state AI safety bill (California's S.B. 1053) and is pushing states to add stronger duties — including backing Massachusetts language requiring independent evaluators for catastrophic-risk assessment — arguing federal law should preempt states only if it's at least as strong. OpenAI is taking the opposite position, lobbying for immediate federal preemption to head off a state-by-state "patchwork" with a single, lighter national standard.
**Why it matters (PM):** The two leading labs are now actively lobbying for opposite regulatory starting points, so the eventual compliance bar is genuinely undecided — worth flagging to legal/compliance now rather than assuming either outcome.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** A state-by-state patchwork (Anthropic's preferred outcome) is disproportionately costly for smaller vendors shipping across states without a compliance team per jurisdiction — worth tracking which way this goes before expanding into new state markets.
**Sources:** [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5918733-openai-anthropic-ai-regulation/) · [Cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-pushes-state-level-ai-safety-laws-diverging-from-openais-national/)
