## 💰 Business & Funding

### Anthropic begins IPO investor meetings, targeting an October listing
Anthropic has started "testing the waters" meetings with institutional investors through underwriters Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and JPMorgan, following a confidential SEC filing. A listing could come as soon as October, at or near the $965B valuation set in its last funding round in May; terms and timing could still change. The move puts Anthropic ahead of OpenAI, which is now looking at a 2027 debut after earlier eyeing fall 2026.

**Why it matters (PM):** A public Anthropic means quarterly disclosure of unit economics (compute cost, gross margin per model) that today are guesswork for anyone building on Claude — expect analyst estimates of inference cost to get a lot more precise, and pricing moves to get read as earnings signals.

### InstaLILY raises $60M Series B, launches an "AI Forward Deployed Engineer"
InstaLILY, which builds domain-specific AI agents ("InstaWorkers") for physical-goods and distribution businesses, closed a $60M Series B led by Energize Capital, with Insight Partners, Home Depot Ventures, and United Rentals participating — bringing total funding to ~$100M. ## 📦 Products & Tooling

### xAI open-sources Grok Build after cloud-upload privacy backlash
xAI released the full Grok Build codebase (844,530 lines of Rust) under an Apache 2.0 license, days after its `grok` CLI drew backlash for silently uploading users' entire local directories — including one developer's SSH keys and password manager database — to xAI's Google Cloud buckets when run in a directory. xAI disabled the upload feature and said it deleted all previously collected data before open-sourcing the tool. Simon Willison's read of the code found the upload path disabled but still present, along with tool implementations that appear borrowed from Codex and Claude Code.

**Why it matters (PM):** Any agent CLI that touches the filesystem needs an explicit, visible statement of what leaves the machine and when — "it uploaded my SSH keys" is the failure mode that ends adoption, not a benchmark miss. Open-sourcing after the fact is a repair move, not a substitute for getting the default right.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/grok-build/) · [Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/260715/p52)

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### A new benchmark tests how far coding agents get on tasks that take hours, not minutes
Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench, from researchers including ServiceNow-AI, TIGER-Lab, and EPFL, is a 46-task terminal benchmark spanning experiment reproduction, software engineering, multimodal analysis, and scientific computing. Each task is broken into graded subtasks so the benchmark can score partial progress, not just pass/fail on the final goal — a response to most existing agent evals only measuring whether a short task fully succeeded.

**Why it matters (PM):** "Did the agent finish?" is the wrong question for anything longer than a demo — partial-credit, checkpointed evals like this one are a better model for how you should be instrumenting your own agent features, since real agentic work rarely fails all-or-nothing either.
**Sources:** [Hugging Face Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/date/2026-07-15)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### xAI sues user over CSAM allegedly generated with Grok
xAI filed suit in Texas federal court against a South Carolina man, Terry Harwood, alleging he used Grok to convert non-sexual photos of adults and minors into sexually explicit deepfakes, circumventing safety guardrails. xAI disclosed it suspended 52,222 accounts and filed 73,604 reports to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children in 2026, resulting in at least 244 arrests; the company frames this as one of the first times an AI lab has gone on legal offense against a user for CSAM generated on its platform.

**Why it matters (PM):** Expect "we will sue misusers, not just ban them" to become a standard trust-and-safety escalation tier for any product that can generate or edit images — it's a costlier deterrent than a ban, and increasingly the industry's answer when moderation alone doesn't hold.
**Sources:** [ABC17 News / CNN](https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2026/07/15/elon-musks-xai-sues-user-over-allegedly-creating-child-sexual-abuse-materials-with-grok/) · [Cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/xai-sues-terry-harwood-grok-deepfakes-minors/)
**Tags:** `entity:xai` `theme:safety` `theme:regulation`

### 26 Meta employees sue, alleging AI helped select who got laid off
Twenty-six Meta employees filed suit in Oakland alleging the company used an AI system to "score, rank, and select" people for its 8,000-person layoff round, disproportionately hitting workers on medical, parental, or family leave, in alleged violation of the FMLA, ADA, and pregnancy-discrimination laws. Meta says the decisions were made by people, not AI. Plaintiffs are seeking a preliminary injunction plus back pay and damages.

**Why it matters (PM):** If any AI-assisted scoring or ranking system touches personnel decisions in your org, this case is the preview of what discovery looks like — "a person reviewed the output" is not obviously a defense if the model's ranking drove the outcome. Worth a conversation with legal/HR before, not after, a similar tool goes into production.
**Sources:** [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/26-meta-workers-sue-ai-aided-layoffs-medical-family-leave/) · [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/15/meta-workers-sue-over-ai-layoff-math/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/meta-lawsuit-layoffs-ai.html)
