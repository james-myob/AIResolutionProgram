## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI makes Codex Goal Mode generally available, ships Appshots and locked-Mac operation

OpenAI moved Codex's Goal Mode from experimental to GA across the app, IDE extension, and CLI — you define an outcome and success criteria and Codex runs toward it for hours or days without further prompting. Two macOS additions land alongside: Appshots (press both Command keys to attach the frontmost app window — screenshot plus text — directly to a Codex thread, no copy-paste) and remote locked-Mac use (Codex can now operate desktop apps on a sleeping or locked Mac via Codex Mobile, with short-lived authorisation, covered displays, and auto-relock on local input).

**Why it matters (PM):** Goal Mode GA marks the transition from Codex as a task-completer to Codex as a long-horizon autonomous agent — the same architectural shift as Anthropic's Outcomes loop, now shipping to general availability in a coding context. The three updates together close the gap between Codex and a developer working unattended overnight.

### OpenAI adds bank account connections to ChatGPT for Pro users

ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US can now connect financial accounts via Plaid, unlocking a Finances dashboard — spending, bills, subscriptions, net worth, and investment data — all queryable in natural language inside ChatGPT. Available on web and iOS. OpenAI frames this as a "personal finance experience" grounded in the user's own financial context, not generic advice.

**Why it matters (PM):** Financial data integration extends ChatGPT from task completion into personal context — it now knows your cash flow alongside your files and calendar, deepening the planning workflows it can meaningfully assist with. Plaid connectivity also signals OpenAI treating data-partnership rails as a first-class product surface.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** For fintech-adjacent or SMB-serving software teams, this is a reference implementation: LLM interfaces are moving toward expecting live financial data connections, not just prompts. If your product sits in the financial workflow of a mid-market business, the baseline for "useful AI" just rose.

### OpenAI files confidential S-1 with the SEC, targeting a Q4 2026 IPO

OpenAI submitted a draft registration statement to the SEC on May 22, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters. ## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Project Glasswing first progress update: AI finds 27-year OpenBSD bug and 16-year FFmpeg vulnerability

Anthropic published the first progress update on Project Glasswing — the initiative that provides over 40 critical infrastructure organisations access to Claude Mythos Preview, its unreleased frontier model, specifically to scan codebases for vulnerabilities before those capabilities become widely available. Confirmed findings include a 27-year-old flaw in OpenBSD and a 16-year-old vulnerability in FFmpeg, alongside multiple Linux kernel exploits. Mythos Preview achieves 83.1% accuracy on cybersecurity vulnerability reproduction benchmarks (vs Opus 4.6's 66.6%). Anthropic has committed $100M in model credits for participants and $4M in open-source donations ($2.5M Linux Foundation, $1.5M Apache Software Foundation).

**Why it matters (PM):** The headline is not the specific bugs — it's the rate and generality of discovery. A model that autonomously finds 27-year-old production OS flaws changes the economics of adversarial access to software vulnerabilities; Glasswing's controlled-defender-first strategy is Anthropic's bet to patch faster than these capabilities can be exploited. If your product ships software with open-source dependencies — which almost all products do — your security posture just changed from theoretical to operational.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Chinese AI models now 61% of OpenRouter token consumption — at 10–30× lower cost

Data from OpenRouter shows Chinese models grew from ~1% of platform token consumption in 2024 to 61% by early 2026, driven primarily by DeepSeek. The cost gap: DeepSeek V4 is priced at $0.0036 per million input tokens vs OpenAI GPT-5.5 at $0.50/M cached — a 140× gap on that comparison; Claude Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.2 are 10–30× more expensive than DeepSeek V3.2 on comparable tasks. Anthropic's own policy paper reportedly acknowledges US frontier models are now "only several months ahead" of Chinese counterparts in capability.

**Why it matters (PM):** The cost gap is a feature-economics problem, not just a geopolitical one. If your product calls an LLM in a loop, the difference between $30 and $0.28 per million output tokens determines whether a feature has viable margins. Chinese models are not yet trusted for regulated enterprise data — but the quality convergence window is narrowing.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-market platforms competing on price face structural pressure: if US enterprise LLMs cost 30× more with diminishing marginal quality advantage, procurement teams will start asking hard questions. Building cost-resilient AI features with provider-agnostic abstraction layers is no longer over-engineering — it is risk management.

### Jack Clark at Oxford: AI Nobel within 12 months, self-improving AI by 2028 — and "non-zero extinction risk"

In a lecture at Oxford University on May 21, Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark predicted AI would collaborate on a Nobel Prize-winning discovery within 12 months, autonomous AI companies generating millions in revenue within 18 months, and AI systems capable of designing their own successors by end of 2028. In the same talk, Clark said AI retains a "non-zero chance of killing everyone on the planet" and that "risk hasn't gone away." Time published a piece today framing the central tension: Anthropic publishes the most credible AI risk warnings in the industry while simultaneously closing a $30B round — and asks whether the two positions are compatible.

**Why it matters (PM):** The 12-month Nobel prediction is specific enough to track against. The Time piece surfaces the harder question any PM should have a position on before a board conversation: does safety credibility co-exist with frontier commercial scale, or does the $900B fundraise structurally change what "safety first" means in practice?
