## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Terra, a cheaper computer-use model
Simon Willison reports that OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra is priced at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens, available in the API and to ChatGPT subscribers. OpenAI describes it as combining detailed visual reasoning with precise computer use. No benchmark scores are on Willison's page; Latent Space's roundup (secondhand) cites an OpenAI claim of parity with GPT-6 Astra on Agents' Last Exam at a tenth of the price, and skeptics say Sonnet 5.5 and Sol are better at similar cost.
**Why it matters (PM):** A mid-priced model tuned for forms, drawings and UI control makes screen-driving agent features cheaper to prototype, but the parity claims are unverified, so run your own evals before repricing anything.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/26/gpt-61-terra/) · [Latent Space (secondhand claims)](https://latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-s1/)

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Anthropic's GSA agreement: 40% off Claude for government, no minimum spend
Anthropic says a new OneGov agreement with the GSA gives government partners 40% off Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the API, with an estimated $1B in savings. It covers US federal agencies and state, local, tribal and territorial governments, with purchases through the GSA Multiple Award Schedule and no minimum commitment. About 500 government organisations already use Claude, Claude for Government is FedRAMP High, and cloud-provider purchasing is to follow in the coming months.
**Why it matters (PM):** Pay-for-what-you-use pricing at a deep discount sets a public reference point for AI contract terms, and FedRAMP-style authorisations are becoming a packaging tier of their own.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Regulated and public-sector-adjacent mid-sized customers will start asking for the same usage-based terms and compliance attestations.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-partnership-gsa)

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Anthropic reports early results from six drug-development partnerships
Anthropic says its life sciences team ran research partnerships with BigHat, Bristol Myers Squibb, Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines and The Column Group. Reported results include a CAR T design about 70% shorter than standard (Generate), about 35,000 antibody designs in a month (Divergence) and an 89% drop in human review time on 1,061 pages of manufacturing documentation, with Claude finding 7 issues reviewers missed (Enveda). Anthropic stresses the work is early and mostly preclinical, and all figures are self-reported.
**Why it matters (PM):** The strongest numbers are in document review and drafting, not discovery, which points to where agent features show measurable time savings first.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Vertical, compliance-heavy document workflows are the same pattern that mid-market software vendors can package.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development) · [Anthropic AI for Science expansion](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ai-for-science-expands-foundations-research-institutions)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### GitHub Octoverse 2026: agentic engineering shows up in the totals
Simon Willison highlights Octoverse figures of about 220 million merged pull requests a month (tripled in under a year), 7.38 billion commits a month and 30.3 million new repositories a month (doubled this year), and attributes the surge to agentic engineering. The attribution is his interpretation; the figures are GitHub's.
**Why it matters (PM):** Code volume is rising far faster than review capacity, so review, testing and provenance tooling is where product gaps are likely to open.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/28/github-octoverse-2026/)
