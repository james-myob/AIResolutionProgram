## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Anthropic Project Glasswing: Claude Mythos finds 10,000+ critical bugs in one month

Anthropic published the first update to Project Glasswing (May 22), its initiative to scan the world's critical software infrastructure before advanced models reach the public. Deploying Claude Mythos Preview to ~50 trusted partners over April–May, the programme uncovered more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities — including an exploit in wolfSSL (a cryptography library present on billions of devices) that could allow certificate forgery. Cloudflare found 2,000 bugs (400 critical/high-severity); Mozilla identified 271 Firefox vulnerabilities compared to 28 found with its previous tooling. The bottleneck has visibly shifted from finding to patching: average time to remediate a high-severity Mythos discovery is two weeks, and some open-source maintainers have asked Anthropic to slow disclosures because patch queues are overloaded. Partners include AWS, Apple, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and the Linux Foundation; Anthropic is committing up to $100M in usage credits to support the work.

**Why it matters (PM):** AI security tooling is now so capable at finding bugs that human patch pipelines are the binding constraint — not detection. If your team uses AI-augmented code scanning, the follow-on question is no longer "is it finding things?" but "can your security team absorb what it finds?" That ratio is the new metric to track.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Most mid-market vendors run software that depends on open-source libraries in the Glasswing scan scope. Upstream hardening from this programme will benefit the whole ecosystem; watch for CVE disclosures from wolfSSL, Firefox, and other scanned projects over the coming weeks and prioritise their dependency updates.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Illinois SB315 passes Senate 52-5: the state AI frontier bill that both OpenAI and Anthropic endorsed

The Illinois Senate passed SB315 on May 22 with a 52-5 vote. The bill targets AI developers with revenues above $500M, requiring them to publish and adhere to a transparency framework covering safety-testing methodology, employ third-party auditors to assess catastrophic-risk capabilities, and report safety incidents. Effective date: 2028. Notably, both OpenAI and Anthropic testified in support, explicitly framing the bill as a step toward a de facto national standard — the same strategy each has applied to comparable legislation in California and New York. SB315 is part of an eight-bill Illinois AI package; implementation deadlines were extended from 2027 to 2028 in recent amendments to allow compliance runway.

**Why it matters (PM):** With no federal framework and Trump's executive order shelved, state-level obligations are the current US compliance calendar: Colorado's high-risk AI law activates June 30, Illinois by 2028. The pattern of lab *support* for these bills is deliberate — large-lab-friendly thresholds ($500M revenue) and federal-standard framing are designed to pre-empt more restrictive state laws. Product teams should track which states are next and whether the thresholds affect their vendor stack.

### VS Code supply chain attack hits GitHub, OpenAI, and Mistral — 3,800 repos stolen

Security group TeamPCP (UNC6780) trojanized the Nx Console VS Code extension (2.2M installs) on May 18; the malicious version was live for approximately 18 minutes before detection. In that window, attackers exfiltrated ~3,800 internal GitHub repositories, 1Password vaults, AWS credentials, and Claude Code configurations. A companion supply chain worm (Mini Shai-Hulud, CVE-2026-45321, CVSS 9.6) propagated through 170+ npm packages and compromised two devices at OpenAI, one at Mistral, and targets at the European Commission. GitHub CISO Alexis Wales confirmed no customer data was affected. OpenAI is revoking its macOS code-signing certificate on June 12 (iOS and Windows certificates already rotated).

**Why it matters (PM):** This is the first documented breach where multiple AI labs' internal tooling was compromised via a developer extension supply chain attack — not a model vulnerability, but the build-time infra wrapping it. Any team where developers use VS Code extensions without explicit vetting is running similar exposure. Extension provenance checks and dependency pinning are now first-class security line items, not background hygiene.

**Sources:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/github-internal-repositories-breached.html) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/github-confirms-3800-repos-stolen-poisoned-vs-code-extension-supply-chain-worm-microsoft-python-sdk)

## 💰 Business & Funding

### Meta's "Model Capability Initiative": employee keystroke logging before the layoffs

New reporting (May 22) reveals Meta deployed surveillance software on US employee laptops in April 2026 — tracking keystrokes, mouse movements, clicks, and periodic screenshots under a programme called the "Model Capability Initiative." The stated purpose: train AI systems to replicate how employees perform digital tasks. The software was live before the May 20 layoff notices; over 1,000 employees signed internal petitions. Meta spokesperson Andy Stone confirmed the data is used for model training, not performance assessment, and that anonymous safeguards are in place. The combination of workflow-capture-then-layoff is the most explicit public example of a company operationalising AI workforce substitution at scale.

**Why it matters (PM):** The practice — capture expert workflows, train models on them, then reduce headcount — is no longer hypothetical; Meta has confirmed it as a live programme. For product teams designing AI features that learn from user actions, the employee-trust and consent architecture this requires is a design problem, not just an HR one.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** The legal and reputational exposure from this approach (even with disclosures) is considerable, and mid-market companies don't have Meta's legal team. If your org is exploring AI workflow capture, the consent and notice framework needs to precede the technical work, not follow it.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Jack Clark's Oxford lecture: 60%+ chance of recursive self-improvement by end 2028

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark lectured at Oxford University this week (covered in Time's "A Tale of Two Anthropics"). His headline claims, each grounded in specific benchmarks: (1) 60%+ probability that an AI model trains its own successor before end 2028, supported by METR time-horizon data showing agent autonomy has scaled from ~30 seconds (GPT-3.5) to ~12 hours (current frontier); (2) AI will co-author a Nobel Prize-worthy discovery within 12 months; (3) AI-only companies generating millions in revenue within 18 months. Clark also acknowledged "a non-zero chance" of extinction-level outcomes — unusually direct public language from a lab co-founder. The Time article frames the broader tension as "two Anthropics": the safety-focused organisation and the one building increasingly capable systems at pace.

**Why it matters (PM):** What's actionable here is not the specific probabilities but the time horizon they imply: if a credible, evidence-citing founder puts >60% on recursive self-improvement within 30 months, "AI improving AI" is a planning assumption, not a fringe scenario. The 12-month Nobel-discovery claim is testable; earmark it to revisit in May 2027.
