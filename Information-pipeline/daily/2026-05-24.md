## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Project Glasswing first-month update: Claude Mythos finds 10,000+ vulnerabilities
Anthropic published its first Glasswing program update on May 22, reporting that Claude Mythos Preview — a frontier model restricted from public availability due to its offensive security capabilities — found over 10,000 high- and critical-severity vulnerabilities across ~50 partner systems in its first month. Cloudflare alone logged 2,000 bugs, 400 critical-path. Most striking: Mythos autonomously constructed a working exploit for wolfSSL, a widely-deployed open-source TLS library, that would let attackers forge SSL certificates and spoof trusted websites. The model also surfaced an estimated 6,200 vulnerabilities across 1,000+ open-source projects, cross-checked by independent security firms.

**Why it matters (PM):** Partners report a 10× improvement in bug-finding rate — not incremental, an order-of-magnitude shift. If you have any product surface that depends on open-source libraries or infrastructure software, your security posture just changed: the question is no longer whether vulnerabilities exist at scale, but whether your team has access to AI-driven tools that find them before attackers do.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-market organisations running standard open-source stacks are the primary beneficiaries of Glasswing's open-source scans — the 6,200 vulnerabilities found in 1,000+ projects will drive patches to software your teams already depend on. No action needed, but worth signalling to your CISO that this class of proactive AI security scanning now exists.

### Anthropic acquires Stainless: SDK infrastructure comes in-house
Announced May 18 but not previously covered: Anthropic acquired Stainless, the 2022-founded startup whose SDK generation platform powered every official Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google API toolkit across TypeScript, Python, Go, and Java. Deal terms were not disclosed by Anthropic; reporting put the price above $300M. Anthropic will wind down all hosted Stainless products — existing SDK customers keep what they've generated, but the hosted generator is going away. The acquisition removes a shared piece of developer infrastructure that Anthropic's two primary competitors also relied on.

**Why it matters (PM):** The SDK is the primary touchpoint between product teams and an AI model API. Anthropic now controls its developer integration layer end-to-end — expect Claude SDKs to evolve toward agent-first conventions (MCP support, structured tool-call patterns) that diverge from OpenAI-compatible designs. If your team is evaluating a multi-provider strategy, this is the moment to audit which provider's SDK you've built on and what migration looks like.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Teams using Stainless's hosted SDK generator for their own API integrations need to plan a migration — the hosted product is going away. Check the wind-down timeline on the Stainless product page.

## 💰 Business & Funding

### Intuit cuts 3,000 jobs (17%) explicitly to fund AI investment
Announced May 20 but not previously covered: Intuit is laying off approximately 3,000 employees — 17% of its 18,200-person global workforce — with US affected staff continuing through July 31. The company simultaneously announced multi-year AI partnerships with OpenAI and Anthropic to embed Claude and ChatGPT into QuickBooks, TurboTax, Credit Karma, and Mailchimp. CEO Sasan Goodarzi's framing was direct: the company projects $500M in annualised cost savings from AI-driven automation, and the headcount reduction frees capital for that investment.

**Why it matters (PM):** Intuit is the clearest mid-market-adjacent data point yet on AI restructuring among software incumbents — not a Big Tech hyperscaler with unlimited capital, but a mature B2B/SMB software company treating AI as a replace-not-augment bet at scale. The Mailchimp and QuickBooks user bases are mid-market bread-and-butter; expect AI-generated financial guidance and automated campaign generation to surface in products your customers already use.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Finance, tax, and marketing tools are about to get AI-native features without a price increase — Intuit is funding the AI build from headcount savings, not a price hike. The near-term product impact: AI-generated financial advice inside QuickBooks and AI-drafted tax summaries in TurboTax arriving within the next 1–2 product cycles.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### EU AI Act Digital Omnibus: high-risk compliance moves to December 2027
The EU's Digital Omnibus legislative package (political agreement May 7) officially delays the compliance deadline for high-risk AI systems from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027 — a 16-month extension. The package also creates a "small mid-cap" relief category for companies with fewer than 750 employees and under €150M annual revenue, giving them simplified documentation templates, lighter quality management standards, and priority access to EU-level regulatory sandboxes. Two deadlines are unchanged: synthetic content marking requirements (deepfakes, AI-generated images/audio) take effect December 2, 2026, and new bans on non-consensual intimate imagery and AI-generated CSAM take effect the same date. The amending regulation must be formally published before August 2, 2026 for the delay to take legal effect.

**Why it matters (PM):** The August 2026 cliff is removed from high-risk AI product roadmaps — but the December 2026 synthetic content marking requirement is not. If your product generates images, video, or audio, you have a 7-month window to comply with marking rules, not 16. The new small mid-cap category is worth reviewing against your organisation's head count and revenue.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Explicit regulatory simplification for sub-750-employee companies changes the compliance cost model. If your team was deferring EU AI Act preparation due to resource constraints, the combination of a later deadline and simplified documentation standards meaningfully changes the trade-off.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### AI GPU demand is crowding consumer RAM out of factories — for years
Simon Willison flagged this on May 22: High-bandwidth memory (HBM) — the specialist chip required for AI training and inference GPUs — is expected to consume approximately 20% of global memory wafer capacity by end of 2026, up from 2% in early 2025. The catch: HBM consumes more than 3× the wafer area per gigabyte compared to standard consumer DRAM. With only three manufacturers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) and an industry pattern of deliberate underproduction to avoid price crashes, constrained consumer RAM supply is now a multi-year structural condition, not a temporary squeeze.

**Why it matters (PM):** AI infrastructure buildout is materially crowding out consumer device supply chains. If your product roadmap includes mobile-first features targeting cost-sensitive emerging markets (Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia), hardware cost headwinds are real and structural — not a 6-month blip.
