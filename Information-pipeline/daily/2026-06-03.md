## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Microsoft launches seven in-house MAI models at Build 2026

Microsoft unveiled a full MAI model family at Build 2026 in San Francisco (June 2–3), led by **MAI-Thinking-1** — a 35-billion-parameter reasoning model with a 256K context window, built without distillation, now in private preview on Foundry. Microsoft says it outperforms Sonnet 4.6 on blind rater preference tests and matches Opus 4.6 on SWE Bench Pro coding tasks at meaningfully lower cost. The full family spans coding (MAI-Code-1, GA in GitHub Copilot and VS Code), image editing (MAI-Image-2.5), transcription across 43 languages (MAI-Transcribe-1.5), and voice in 15+ new languages (MAI-Voice-2); all will be available on Fireworks AI, Baseten, and OpenRouter alongside Microsoft Foundry.

**Why it matters (PM):** For any product built on Azure or GitHub, MAI-Thinking-1 introduces a credible second reasoning model in the existing toolchain — specifying models explicitly in your architecture, rather than hard-coding to an OpenAI default, is now a practical near-term decision, not a theoretical one.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI Codex expands beyond developers — roles, Sites, and Annotations

OpenAI announced three additions to Codex on June 2: role-specific plugins (six bundles covering analysts, marketers, investors, operators, designers, and researchers — combining 62 apps and 110 skills), Sites (in preview for business/enterprise users; Codex-generated interactive dashboards, project boards, and lightweight web apps shared by URL), and Annotations (inline document, spreadsheet, and slide editing beyond code). Context: 5 million people now use Codex weekly, and non-developer users — growing more than 3x faster than developers — make up 20% of that base. Codex also launched on AWS via Amazon Bedrock, available for commercial and GovCloud regions.

**Why it matters (PM):** The 3x non-developer growth rate signals Codex is on a trajectory to become the AI operating layer for office work broadly — if your product targets any of the analyst, marketer, or operator persona, there is now an Altman-backed feature set directly in that space.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** The AWS Bedrock availability means IT teams can offer structured Codex access to non-technical staff inside existing AWS agreements without individual OpenAI accounts — watch for bundled enterprise pricing to put pressure on point-solution AI tools at renewal time.

### Anthropic formalises the Claude partner ecosystem with a tiered Services Track

Anthropic launched the Services Track and Partner Hub of the Claude Partner Network on June 3, creating three tiers with verifiable, daily-refreshed requirements: Select (10+ certified staff, 2+ production deployments, 1 public customer story), Preferred (100+ staff, 15+ customers, 3 stories), and Global Premier (1,000+ staff, 100+ customers across 3 regions, 15 stories plus executive partnership access). **Why it matters (PM):** The tier structure creates accountability pressure on SIs and consultancies to demonstrate production deployments, not just training hours — when evaluating a partner for a Claude project, tier level is now a meaningful proxy for real delivery track record, not just spend.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** The Select tier is achievable by regional boutique firms (10 certified, 2 live deployments), creating a searchable directory of qualified AI implementation partners for smaller mid-market buyers who don't have relationships with the Big 4.

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Anthropic maps a year of AI-enabled cyber threats — medium-or-higher risk actors up 70%

Anthropic released a year-long analysis of 832 malicious accounts banned between March 2025 and March 2026, mapped against the MITRE ATT&CK framework and contributed to Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report. The headline finding: the share of banned actors classified as medium-risk or higher jumped from 33% in the first six months to 56% in the second — a roughly 1.7x escalation — with AI applied most heavily at the later, more damaging attack phases (lateral movement, persistence) rather than initial access. The report was released alongside an expansion of Project Glasswing — Anthropic's AI-assisted vulnerability scanning program — to 150 new organisations across 15+ countries including power, water, healthcare, and communications infrastructure.

**Why it matters (PM):** AI is making attackers more capable at the stages that cause the most damage (post-access lateral movement), not just noisier at the perimeter. For product teams building agentic features or exposing AI APIs: the attack surface is shifting toward model-assisted privilege escalation — worth one explicit conversation with your security team about agent permissions scoping and API key lifecycle.

**Sources:** [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/news/AI-enabled-cyber-threats-mitre-attack) · [Anthropic Glasswing expansion](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-project-glasswing) · [Industrial Cyber](https://industrialcyber.co/ai/anthropic-flags-ai-driven-cyberattacks-warns-that-cybersecurity-has-reached-a-critical-inflection-point/)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Colorado's June 30 AI compliance deadline is cancelled — new law takes effect January 2027

A federal magistrate stayed enforcement of Colorado's original comprehensive AI law (SB 24-205) on April 27, with the DOJ and xAI jointly filing suit over its constitutionality. The Colorado legislature simultaneously passed a replacement (SB 26-189), signed by Governor Polis on May 14, that drops the original law's risk management programs, annual impact assessments, and algorithmic discrimination duties in favour of a narrower notice-and-transparency framework. The new effective date is January 1, 2027, with enforcement beginning only after the attorney general completes required rulemaking.

**Why it matters (PM):** The May 21 briefing flagged Colorado's June 30 deadline as the operative US AI compliance date — that deadline no longer exists. If your compliance roadmap had June 30 gating any feature launches or AI disclosures, recalibrate to January 2027; retain the work on consumer notice and appeal rights (which SB 26-189 preserves), and note that no preemptive federal US AI law exists — state-by-state variance remains the planning environment.

**Sources:** [Hunton Privacy blog](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/colorado-ai-act-amended-and-effective-date-delayed) · [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/colorado-amends-its-artificial-intelligence-law-substantially-reducing) · [Law and the Workplace](https://www.lawandtheworkplace.com/2026/04/colorado-takes-a-major-step-towards-rewriting-its-ai-law-as-its-effective-date-approaches/)
