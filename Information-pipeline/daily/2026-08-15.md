## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### xAI ships Grok 4.6, the cheapest model at the intelligence frontier
xAI released Grok 4.6 on 12 August, built for long-running agent tasks, agentic coding, and interactive/visual work. It ties OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (one point behind Anthropic's Claude Fable 5) with a 500K-token context window, at the same headline price as Grok 4.5: $2/$6 per million input/output tokens. The catch: once a prompt crosses 200K tokens, the rate doubles to $4/$12 per million for the entire request, not just the overflow — an easy detail to miss when comparing sticker prices.

**Why it matters (PM):** Frontier-tier intelligence is now available at a genuinely lower price point, but only if your prompts stay under the 200K-token threshold — model-routing logic that only checks the headline rate will misprice long-context workloads.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** A frontier-capable model priced this aggressively lowers the cost floor for smaller teams building agentic features, provided they design prompts to stay inside the cheap-tier context band.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI expands Daybreak with GPT-5.6-Cyber, a model tuned to stop refusing offensive-security work
OpenAI widened its Daybreak cybersecurity program into two tiers — Daybreak Blue for general defensive use (built on GPT-5.6 Sol) and Daybreak Red, which gives vetted partners (Accenture, IBM, CrowdStrike, Cisco, Sophos, Cloudflare) access to GPT-5.6-Cyber, trained for vulnerability research and exploit-chain development. In OpenAI's internal evaluation, GPT-5.6-Cyber completed 95% of advanced cybersecurity prompts (exploit chains, auth bypass, privilege escalation) versus 1.5% for the standard GPT-5.6 Sol.

**Why it matters (PM):** This is a template worth watching beyond security: a lab shipping the same base model twice, with the "unlocked" version gated by partner vetting rather than a model-level capability change — expect similar dual-tier access patterns in other high-risk domains (bio, finance) as labs look for a middle ground between "refuse everything" and "refuse nothing."
**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-daybreak-as-the-cyber-defense-window-narrows/) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-launches-gpt-56-cyber-with.html) · [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/08/11/openai-expands-daybreak-with-new-gpt-5-6-cyber-model/)

## 💰 Business & Funding

### Vibe-coding platform Lovable doubles valuation to $13.3B; AI-agent-security startup Zenity raises $125M
Swedish "vibe-coding" startup Lovable raised a $400M Series C led by Menlo Ventures and the Scaleup Europe Fund, confirming a $13.3B valuation — roughly double its prior mark — after crossing $500M in annualized revenue in June. Separately, Zenity, which sells a platform for securing and governing AI agents, raised a $125M Series C led by Norwest Venture Partners, bringing its total funding to about $185M.

**Why it matters (PM):** The two rounds sit on either side of the same shift: more software gets built by non-engineers via natural-language tools, and a parallel governance-tooling market is scaling to manage the agents that result — worth having both categories on your radar if your product touches either side.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Lovable's growth is a direct signal that mid-market and SMB teams are increasingly building internal tools themselves rather than buying or commissioning them — a real substitution risk for vertical SaaS aimed at that segment.

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### Google DeepMind's own AI safety team had to work around its AI resume-screener
Bloomberg obtained an internal memo from Google DeepMind's AGI Safety and Alignment team — the group hiring specifically to reduce AI-related risk — telling applicants to also fill out a separate, human-reviewed form because the standard applicant-tracking system carries "a non-trivial probability" of incorrectly screening out a qualified CV or losing it in transit.

**Why it matters (PM):** If the team most incentivized to get this right still doesn't trust its own AI-driven screening pipeline, treat that as a live data point, not a hypothetical, before shipping (or trusting) any AI system that makes irreversible pass/fail calls on people — audit for silent false negatives, not just headline accuracy.
**Sources:** [Futurism](https://futurism.com/future-society/google-hr-resume-applicants-deepmind-ai-hiring) · [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AyDNvb3Pw6Kgo7Dqb/the-agi-safety-and-alignment-team-at-google-deepmind-is)
