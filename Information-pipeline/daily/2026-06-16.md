## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### 100+ cybersecurity experts demand the US lift Fable 5 and Mythos export restrictions

An open letter organised by former Facebook security chief Alex Stamos — now signed by more than 100 practitioners from Nvidia, Google, Adobe, Zoom, and Sophos — was published June 16, asking the Commerce Department to rescind the export control directive that suspended Fable 5 and Mythos 5 on June 12. The letter's core argument: the "jailbreak" that triggered the ban (asking the model to "fix this code") is standard defensive cybersecurity workflow, not a bypass. Security researcher Kate Moussouris, whose work on offensive/defensive equivalence was cited in the government's rationale, clarified publicly that her research was conducted for defence purposes — the government's invocation of it misrepresents the original intent. Anthropic's CFO has stated that the blacklisting could reduce 2026 revenue by multiple billions; litigation against the Pentagon is active, with senior Anthropic researchers dispatched to Commerce Department meetings this week.

**Why it matters (PM):** Fable 5's three-day deployment window (launched June 9, suspended June 12) is the clearest demonstration yet that frontier model APIs are not reliable infrastructure. Enterprise teams need a multi-vendor fallback strategy; single-model dependencies are now an explicit operational risk, not just a procurement checkbox.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Any product that integrated Fable 5 between June 9-12 is dealing with a live dependency failure. The episode converts "AI vendor concentration risk" from a theoretical procurement concern into a real one with a documented failure case to cite.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Daniela Amodei at Bloomberg Tech: the IPO is inevitable — and Goldman Sachs explains why

At Bloomberg Tech on June 15, Anthropic's president made the most explicit public case yet for going public: frontier AI requires sustained infrastructure capital at a scale that venture cannot provide. Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $47 billion in May 2026 (up from ~$9B at year-end 2025), yet the company spends approximately $1.25B/month on compute — primarily through its xAI/SpaceX Colossus 1 agreement — leaving thin operating headroom even at current revenue. Goldman Sachs published a research note forecasting $7.6 trillion in cumulative global AI infrastructure investment between 2026 and 2031, equivalent to roughly 25% of annual US GDP. Both Anthropic (S-1 filed June 1, targeting ~$965B valuation) and OpenAI (S-1 filed June 8, Goldman Sachs/Morgan Stanley, September IPO target) moving to public markets within two weeks of each other is the structural signal: frontier AI is graduating from venture-backed experiment to public infrastructure.

**Why it matters (PM):** Public-company economics impose margin and pricing discipline. Labs that have been pricing models at or below cost to drive adoption will face shareholder pressure; any AI product cost model built now should include a +20% sensitivity scenario for input costs over 12-18 months.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-market procurement decisions made against today's frontier AI pricing need a 12-24 month sensitivity analysis. Labs as public companies will have less flexibility to subsidise growth-stage pricing; lock-in pricing windows while they're still on offer.

## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Fable 5's "Pack Hunt" attack and the 120,000-character system prompt leak

Technical analysis published June 15 explains what happened in the 48 hours after Fable 5 launched. Security researcher "Pliny the Liberator" used a method called "Pack Hunt": harmful requests were decomposed into individually-benign sub-questions via Unicode substitution and narrative framing, keeping each below classifier thresholds before reassembling outputs — a sophisticated multi-step technique, not a simple one-click bypass. More consequentially, Pliny published approximately 120,000 characters of Fable 5's internal system prompt to GitHub, revealing that Anthropic's safety layer is built on natural-language instructions at the prompt level rather than hard-coded refusals baked into model weights. Security practitioners note this structural fact applies to every frontier model, not just Fable 5.

**Why it matters (PM):** System-prompt-based safety is a soft boundary: it can be read out, mapped, and worked around by a determined attacker. Teams building compliance-sensitive products on frontier models should treat the system-prompt layer as a necessary-but-not-sufficient control — and plan their safety architecture accordingly.
