## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2
Anthropic says Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are the same model: Fable has stronger cyber and biology safeguards and is open to all users, while Mythos goes to vetted organisations via its Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs. Anthropic reports 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at Xhigh effort (about level with Opus 5.5 at High effort for roughly half the cost per task), 69.9% on FrontierSWE v2 (vs 62.3% for Opus 5.5), 89.3% on OSWorld 2.1 and 74.2% on Humanity's Last Exam with tools; outputs are up to 30% faster than Fable 5.1. Pricing is $8/$40 per million input/output tokens, with an optional fast mode (up to 2.5x faster) at $16/$80; the API ID is `claude-fable-5-2`. These are Anthropic's own benchmark numbers, with external testing from UK AISI, METR and others noted in the post.
**Why it matters (PM):** The cost-per-task claim against Opus 5.5 gives you a concrete reason to re-run your model-selection and unit-economics sheet for any coding or agent feature this week.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Available on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure, so mid-market customers can adopt it inside existing cloud contracts without a new vendor review.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)
**Tags:** `entity:anthropic` `entity:claude-fable-5` `theme:ai-cost` `theme:agents` `lens:mid-market` `tier:t1`

### Fable 5.2 loosens its cyber and biology classifiers
Anthropic says Fable 5.2's classifiers block about 55% fewer requests than Fable 5.1; blocked queries fall back to Opus 4.8 (cyber) or Opus 5 (biology). It also reports its strongest anti-distillation protections yet, cutting harvested data by 90–99%, and notes the models voice suspicion of being evaluated more often than recent models, which complicates safety testing.
**Why it matters (PM):** If you deferred security or life-science features because of over-refusals, the general-access tier is worth retesting.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)

### Hide agent-written PR descriptions behind `<details>`
Simon Willison proposes keeping a short human-written opener visible and collapsing long agent-generated PR text inside HTML `<details><summary>`, with the model name in the summary.
**Why it matters (PM):** A cheap review-hygiene convention for teams whose engineers now ship agent-authored PRs.
