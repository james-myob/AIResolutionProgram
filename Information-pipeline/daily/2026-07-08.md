## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 as three explicitly tiered models
OpenAI is publicly rolling out GPT-5.6 on July 9 as three variants — Sol (flagship), Terra (~GPT-5.5-level performance at roughly half the cost), and Luna (lowest-cost tier) — priced at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 per million input/output tokens. The family had been restricted to US-government-approved organisations since a limited release the prior week.
**Why it matters (PM):** "Which OpenAI model" is now itself a cost-vs-capability decision spelled out in the pricing table — benchmark all three against your actual task mix before defaulting to the flagship.
**Sources:** [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-360/) · [TLDR AI](https://tldr.tech/ai/2026-07-08)
**Tags:** `entity:openai` `entity:gpt-5-6` `theme:pricing`

### xAI ships Grok 4.5, co-developed with Cursor for coding
xAI released Grok 4.5, a coding- and agentic-engineering-focused flagship built jointly with Cursor, served at 80 tokens/second and priced at $2/$6 per million input/output tokens. It's live in Grok Build, Cursor, and the xAI console; EU availability is delayed to mid-July.
**Why it matters (PM):** A lab co-designing a model directly with a named IDE/agent vendor — rather than shipping a general API and letting tools adapt — is a distribution pattern worth watching for elsewhere.

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Anthropic finds a hidden internal "workspace" where Claude manipulates concepts before answering
Anthropic researchers identified "J-space" — an internal representation where Claude appears to hold and manipulate concepts before producing its final output. Swapping a hidden signal (e.g. "spider" for "ant") changes the answer accordingly, and suppressing J-space activity degrades performance on complex reasoning tasks.
**Why it matters (PM):** Early empirical evidence that frontier models have identifiable internal reasoning structure — the kind of finding that eventually underpins interpretability tooling and vendor claims about steering or auditing model behaviour, relevant to any future compliance conversation.
**Sources:** [The Neuron Daily](https://www.theneurondaily.com/p/anthropic-found-claude-s-hidden-workspace) · [TLDR AI](https://tldr.tech/ai/2026-07-07) · [The Rundown AI](https://www.therundown.ai/p/the-part-of-claude-s-brain-nobody-built)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### a16z: enterprise software is losing its "head" to AI agents
Tied to Salesforce's "Headless 360" announcement, a16z's Seema Amble (with Steven Sinofsky and Elena Burger) argues that as AI agents become the primary "users" of enterprise software, value shifts from the UI layer to the underlying data/API layer — making incumbents like SAP and Salesforce harder to displace, not easier, contrary to the standard "AI kills SaaS" narrative.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product's moat is mostly its interface, this directly challenges that assumption — durable value may be moving to whoever owns the workflow data and API surface an agent actually calls.

## 📒 New Concepts

### Headless Software
**Plain English:** As AI agents (not humans clicking through screens) become the primary "users" of enterprise software, the software's real value moves away from its user interface toward the underlying data model and API surface those agents actually call. This runs counter to the popular "AI kills the SaaS UI" narrative — it implies whoever already owns the data/API layer, often the incumbent, may be better positioned to capture agent-driven value than a new UI-first challenger.
**Origin:** [a16z — Seema Amble, "Is Software Losing Its Head?"](https://www.a16z.news/p/is-software-losing-its-head)
**Tag:** `concept:headless-software`
