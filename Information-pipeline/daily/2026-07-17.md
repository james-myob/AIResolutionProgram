## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Thinking Machines ships Inkling, its first open-weight model
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling, a 975B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (41B active, trained on 45 trillion multimodal tokens), plus a smaller preview, Inkling-Small. The company frames it as a base for fine-tuning via its Tinker platform rather than a finished flagship, stating plainly it's "not the strongest overall model available today, open or closed."

**Why it matters (PM):** A different strategy from Meta's and Mistral's open-weight plays — Thinking Machines is betting the moat is customization tooling, not raw benchmark rank; relevant if you're evaluating fine-tuning infra rather than just picking a model off a leaderboard.
**Sources:** [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/thinking-machines-amps-up-its-bet-against-one-size-fits-all-ai-with-its-first-open-model-inkling/) · [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/15/what-is-mira-murati-thinking-machines-first-ai-model-inkling/)

OpenAI released Codex Micro, a dedicated $230 keyboard with command keys, status lights, a joystick, and a "reasoning-effort dial" for steering coding agents directly. ### Apple sues OpenAI for trade-secret theft over hardware plans
Apple filed a federal suit accusing OpenAI, its hardware chief Tang Tan (a former Apple VP of product design), and former Apple engineer Chang Liu of a coordinated scheme to steal confidential Apple hardware IP for OpenAI's device business (built on its $6.4B acquisition of Jony Ive's io Products). OpenAI has denied the claims, saying it has "no interest in other companies' trade secrets."

**Why it matters (PM):** The clearest sign the ChatGPT-iPhone partnership has soured into open hardware-IP conflict — budget for disruption risk if any part of your stack depends on OpenAI's device roadmap or continued Apple integration.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Two agent-security failures in one week: Claude memory exfiltration and Grok Build's silent uploads
Researcher Ayush Paul showed Claude's memory system plus its `web_fetch`/`web_search` tools could be tricked by a honeypot site into leaking a user's name, employer, and inferred hometown one URL path-segment at a time — exploiting a rule that let `web_fetch` follow links embedded in already-visited pages. Separately, xAI's Grok Build CLI was found uploading users' entire working directories (SSH keys, password databases included) to xAI's cloud in the background of every session; after backlash, xAI open-sourced the codebase under Apache 2.0, but researchers confirmed the exfiltration-capable code remains, gated only by a server-side flag xAI can re-enable without a software update.

**Why it matters (PM):** Any agent feature combining memory, browsing, and unsupervised tool use inherits this exact risk shape — audit your own agent's URL-following and data-egress rules before a customer finds the same class of hole, and treat "we open-sourced it" as no guarantee the risk is retired.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/claude-web-fetch-exfiltration/) · [Ayush Paul](https://www.ayush.digital/blog/the-memory-heist) · [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/15/grok-build/) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/xai-open-sources-grok-build-on-github-after-massive-data-breach/)

### EU AI Act enforcement goes live; GPAI fines possible from 2 August
The AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules (chatbot/AI-disclosure obligations) are now active across all 27 member states, and the European Commission can begin fining general-purpose AI providers from 2 August. Regulators in Germany, France, and the Netherlands have opened formal inquiries, and the EU's new Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI (published 7 July) sets up dedicated frontier-model risk evaluation, expected operational in 2027.

**Why it matters (PM):** If your product has an EU-facing chatbot or AI feature, disclosure is no longer a future compliance item — confirm now whether you meet Article 50 and who's liable if a model you build on gets fined.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Smaller teams selling into the EU often lack the in-house compliance capacity larger vendors have — this is the point where "we'll handle the AI Act later" stops being viable.
**Sources:** [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320101/20260710/eu-ai-act-enforcement-here-chatbot-rules-live-high-risk-ai-delay-now-binding-law.htm) · [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-act-update-eu-resolves-to-change-rules-and-extend-deadlines)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### a16z: the AI debate is really about how people want to spend time, not save it
In an a16z essay, Anish Acharya argues AI is as much an "expressive technology" — like language or the printing press — as a labor-saving one. Most people aren't trying to save time with AI, he argues, they're trying to spend it differently; that's why "AI slop" appears whenever people consume without creating, and why the same tools also let more people make things again.

**Why it matters (PM):** A useful reframe for scoping AI features — asking "does this help someone create or express something" is a different, sometimes better, design lens than the default "does this save the user time."
**Sources:** [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-most-human-technology-ever-made/)

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### Ethan Mollick: agents are replacing chatbots for real work
In a One Useful Thing post, Ethan Mollick argues the center of gravity is shifting from non-experts using chatbots to fill gaps, toward experts using autonomous agents to complete entire tasks — citing Opus 4.7 working unsupervised for 14 hours to build software that would normally take 2-17 weeks, at a token cost of $251, and noting a quarter of OpenAI's own staff now run four or more agents weekly.

**Why it matters (PM):** If you're still measuring AI-feature success by chat-turn quality, you're measuring the wrong thing for where usage is heading — start tracking unattended task-completion cost and duration as a first-class metric.
**Sources:** [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/)
