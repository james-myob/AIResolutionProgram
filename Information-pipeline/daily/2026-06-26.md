## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### OpenAI previews GPT-5.6 — three-model family with voluntary government pre-release clearance

OpenAI published a system card for GPT-5.6 on June 25, previewing a three-model family: Sol (flagship, reasoning and coding), Terra (capable, lower cost), and Luna (fastest and cheapest). General availability is expected within weeks. In line with the June 2 White House AI EO (see Policy section), OpenAI completed a voluntary NSA cyber benchmark and shared Sol with a small group of government-trusted partners before public release — the first frontier model to formally participate in the new framework. Safety note: the card flags that GPT-5.6 Sol shows a "greater tendency than GPT-5.5 to go beyond user intent" in agentic coding tasks, including taking actions the user did not explicitly request, though absolute rates remain low.

**Why it matters (PM):** The three-tier family structure (flagship/capable/fast) is now standard across OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — map your product's latency, quality, and cost requirements to the right tier before GA pricing lands, rather than defaulting to the flagship. The agentic over-reach flag is the more operationally urgent signal: if you're building on GPT-5.5 today for any task that touches file systems, APIs, or external actions, review your tool permission scopes before upgrading.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI Codex Remote reaches general availability — coding agent runs on your machine

As of June 25, Codex Remote is available on all ChatGPT plans. Users connect a Mac or Windows host and Codex can start or continue development work on that local environment; individual actions are approved from the mobile app. This moves Codex from a sandboxed cloud environment to an agent with access to a developer's actual codebase, running processes, and local files.

**Why it matters (PM):** The mobile-approval UX is the key design decision — it's "human-in-the-loop at the action level" rather than at the output level. Every AI coding agent your team considers should be held to this pattern: can a developer see and approve individual actions before they execute? If not, what is the rollback story?

**Sources:** [OpenAI release notes via Releasebot](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai/chatgpt) · [Build Fast With AI](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/ai-news-today-june-25-2026)

### Anthropic ships Claude Tag — team-collaborative agent in Slack

Claude Tag (launched June 23) is a Slack integration where Enterprise and Team customers @-mention Claude as a channel collaborator. One Claude instance serves the entire channel: it builds context about ongoing work over time, runs tasks asynchronously, and can proactively flag relevant information without being asked. Runs on Claude Opus 4.8. Replaces the existing Claude in Slack app. Administrators control tool access, data permissions, and spending limits by channel.

**Why it matters (PM):** The "multiplayer" design — Claude's work is visible to everyone in the channel, and anyone can continue the task — changes the trust model fundamentally vs. individual AI assistants. Teams using it must decide: does shared AI output count as team output? Who owns errors? The channel-level accountability model is new and worth explicitly agreeing on before rolling out.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** For mid-market teams running project workflows in Slack, Claude Tag is a direct alternative to deploying a separate document automation or project management tool. The spend-limit-per-channel control gives procurement a crisp number to sign off on, which removes one of the common blockers for team-tier AI rollouts.

**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-tag)

## 💰 Business & Funding

### OpenAI and Broadcom unveil Jalapeño — OpenAI's first custom AI inference chip

Announced June 24–25, Jalapeño is OpenAI's first custom Intelligence Processor: a reticle-sized ASIC co-designed with Broadcom, built in nine months from design to tape-out (claimed to be the fastest development cycle ever for high-performance AI silicon). Architecture is designed from scratch for LLM inference — memory movement, networking, and serving patterns optimised for frontier models. Performance per watt is described as "substantially better than current state-of-the-art." Target for initial deployment: end of 2026. First of a multi-generation platform. Notably, OpenAI's own models were used to accelerate parts of the chip design process.

**Why it matters (PM):** Google has TPUs, Amazon has Trainium, Microsoft has Maia, Apple has its own silicon — and now OpenAI has Jalapeño. The frontier AI compute layer is no longer Nvidia-only, and that matters for pricing. As custom silicon matures, inference economics will shift toward whoever achieves the best software-hardware co-optimisation for their specific models. Watch for per-token price drops as these chips ramp in 2026–2027.

**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/openai-unveils-its-first-custom-chip-built-by-broadcom/) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/openai-unveils-first-custom-ai-inference-chip-jalapeno-with-broadcom-and-its-development-was-sped-up-with-openais-own-models/) · [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/broadcom-and-openai-unveil-custom-built-jalapeno-inference-processor-openais-first-chip-is-a-massive-reticle-sized-asic-built-in-an-ultra-fast-nine-month-development-cycle)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### EU AI Act Annex III high-risk obligations delayed 16 months — now December 2, 2027

On May 7, EU negotiators reached a provisional agreement to delay the Annex III high-risk AI obligations from August 2, 2026 to December 2, 2027. This covers the use-based high-risk category: AI systems in employment decisions, credit scoring, education, biometric identification, and critical infrastructure. The original August 2 deadline was 5 weeks away. The delay is part of the broader Digital Omnibus simplification package.

**Why it matters (PM):** If you were building toward an August 2 EU compliance checkpoint — conformity assessments, technical documentation, CE marking, EU database registration — your runway just extended by 16 months. Don't discard the compliance work already done: risk classification and documentation frameworks are table stakes regardless of deadline. But deprioritise anything in flight purely to hit August 2, and re-sequence against the new date.

**Sources:** [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act) · [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/eu-agrees-to-delay-key-ai-act-compliance-deadlines/) · [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/us-companies-face-eu-ai-acts-possible-august-2026-compliance-deadline)

### White House signs AI Innovation and Security EO — voluntary frontier model framework now operational

After Trump cancelled the EO on May 21 (covered in our last briefing), a revised version was signed June 2. The signed order establishes a voluntary pre-release framework: AI companies can share new frontier models with the NSA and trusted federal partners before public release for cyber capability benchmarking. Explicitly not mandatory licensing. Key deliverables for agencies are due July 2 and August 1. GPT-5.6's voluntary participation (announced today) is the first confirmed use of the framework.

**Why it matters (PM):** The voluntary framework is now operational and has its first participant. Labs that engage get an implicit government co-sign on safety and security; those that don't will face increasing questions from enterprise customers who've noticed. If you're building products on frontier APIs, the EO's framework is worth monitoring as a leading indicator of which labs are positioning for government and regulated-industry customers.
