## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### MiniMax open-sources H3, a 42.5GB omni-modal video model
MiniMax shipped H3 in its API on 31 July and followed through on 3 August by open-weighting it under the MiniMax H3 Community License (four countries excluded from the license). The model generates 15-second, 2K-resolution clips with native stereo audio, and picked up native ComfyUI support the same day.

**Why it matters (PM):** A frontier-adjacent video model with open weights lowers the cost floor for any team weighing build-vs-buy on video generation — worth a bench test before committing to a closed API vendor for that use case.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Luna price 80%, Terra 20%
OpenAI dropped Luna pricing to $0.20/$1.20 per million input/output tokens (down 80%) and Terra to $2/$12 (down 20%), effective 1 August for all customers, with pre-purchased credits honored at old ratios for a two-week grace period. Sol, the top-tier model, was untouched. OpenAI attributed the cut to efficiency gains from building GPT-5.6 itself.

**Why it matters (PM):** If you scoped a Luna-tier feature on last month's pricing, your unit economics just improved — but re-run the numbers rather than assume, since Terra's smaller cut means the tier boundaries moved too.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** An 80% cut on the cheapest tier makes always-on AI features (support triage, drafting, summarization) newly affordable for teams previously priced out of frontier-adjacent quality — worth revisiting any build decision made on the old Luna pricing.
**Sources:** [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/open-ai-price-cut-gpt.html) · [StartupHub.ai](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/artificial-intelligence/2026/openai-slashes-gpt-5-6-prices-in-efficiency-push)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### EU delays AI Act's high-risk rules to 2027-28, but AI-disclosure rules are enforceable now
Under the Digital Omnibus on AI signed 8 July, the EU pushed back the AI Act's high-risk system obligations — standalone Annex III systems now have until 2 December 2027, and AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) until 2 August 2028. What did land on schedule, 2 August: Article 50 transparency rules (chatbots and AI-generated content must identify themselves as AI) and the European Commission's power to investigate and fine general-purpose AI providers.

**Why it matters (PM):** The compliance deadline most teams were building toward just moved out 12-24 months for high-risk classification work — don't stop that work, but you likely have runway to sequence it behind other roadmap priorities. The disclosure requirement is live today, though, so check any chatbot or generated-content surface serving EU users for an AI-disclosure label.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Smaller vendors with thinner compliance teams get real breathing room on the expensive part (risk management, conformity assessment) while still needing to solve the cheap part (disclosure labeling) immediately — reprioritize accordingly rather than treating the Act as one single deadline.
**Sources:** [Goodwin Law](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force) · [Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/07/the-eu-ai-act-when-does-it-become-enforceable-now/) · [aiacto.eu](https://www.aiacto.eu/en/blog/ai-act-what-changes-august-2-2026)
