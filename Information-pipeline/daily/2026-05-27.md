## 💰 Business & Funding

### Anthropic closes $30B+ round at ~$900B valuation — world's most valuable private AI company

Anthropic's oversubscribed funding round — more than $30 billion at a valuation above $900 billion — closed this week, surpassing OpenAI's $852 billion mark from March. The round is co-led by Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer, Altimeter, and Greenoaks (each contributing roughly $2 billion), with existing backers Founders Fund and General Catalyst also participating. Anthropic projects $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue — more than double the prior quarter — and expects its first profitable quarter.

**Why it matters (PM):** This is not speculative valuation; it is enterprise-grade coding agent revenue at scale. The pricing floor for frontier models is being set in these enterprise contracts right now — any cost model built before 2026 is stale.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** With a public offering reportedly targeting late 2026, expect Anthropic's product and pricing strategy to shift toward demonstrability: more packaged tiers, more predictable SLAs, stronger enterprise commitments — all improving leverage for mid-market buyers negotiating contracts this year.

### Cognition raises $1B at $26B valuation — Devin hits $492M ARR with 50% MoM enterprise growth

Cognition AI, maker of AI software engineer Devin, raised over $1 billion at a $26 billion post-money valuation, more than doubling its $10.2 billion mark from eight months prior. Lux Capital and General Catalyst co-led, joined by returning investors Founders Fund and 8VC plus newcomers Ribbit Capital and Atreides. Enterprise usage of Devin has grown 50% month-over-month for six consecutive months; clients include Mercedes-Benz, NASA, Goldman Sachs, and Santander, with $492 million in annualized revenue run-rate.

**Why it matters (PM):** $492M ARR from an agent that autonomously writes and ships production code means "AI codes for us" has graduated from experiment to line-item. Any team still treating AI coding agents as exploratory tooling should revisit build-vs-buy and tooling decisions made 12 months ago.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Enterprise adoption at Goldman, Mercedes-Benz, and the US military demonstrates autonomous coding agents are passing procurement review in regulated, complex environments. Mid-market teams with simpler security profiles should have a faster path to comparable deployment.

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Project Glasswing milestone: Claude Mythos identifies 10,000+ critical software flaws; Claude Security hits public beta

An update from Anthropic's Project Glasswing, published May 26, reports that Claude Mythos Preview has identified more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities across widely deployed internet software in its first deployment weeks. Roughly 50 partner organizations are running Mythos in controlled defensive security workflows: Cloudflare reported 2,000 bugs (400 rated high/critical), Mozilla found and fixed 271 vulnerabilities in Firefox 150. Alongside this, Claude Security — an enterprise beta that lets teams scan codebases and triage vulnerabilities via Claude Opus 4.7 — has patched more than 2,100 vulnerabilities in its first three weeks.

**Why it matters (PM):** AI-assisted vulnerability scanning is credibly deployed at infrastructure level, not just in security demos. For teams whose products depend on shared open-source libraries, Glasswing may have already surfaced a bug in your dependency tree before you knew to look.

**Why it matters (mid-market):** Claude Security's public beta removes the specialist-security-team barrier for smaller organisations — mid-market teams get model-assisted triage without running a dedicated red team, a meaningful shift in the cost structure of keeping software secure.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### China expands AI talent travel restrictions to private firms — Alibaba and DeepSeek researchers now need pre-approval

China has extended restrictions on overseas travel for top AI researchers to employees at private companies including Alibaba and DeepSeek, Bloomberg reported on May 26. Where previously some AI engineers only needed to report overseas travel, the new rules require active pre-approval before international trips, bringing leading AI researchers under restrictions previously reserved for nuclear scientists and state-owned enterprise executives. The policy reflects Beijing's view that frontier AI talent is a national-security asset on par with classified technical knowledge.

**Why it matters (PM):** Any product team recruiting Chinese AI researchers, co-authoring papers with Chinese institutions, or depending on talent from DeepSeek/Alibaba spinoffs should expect this to add friction to international collaboration, conference attendance, and secondments — and factor that into hiring and partnership timelines now.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Demis Hassabis says AGI arrives around 2029–2030; frames today's agents as the "practice run"

In an Axios interview published May 26, DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis gave his most specific public AGI timeline to date: he sees AGI as achievable around 2030, with 2029 as his earlier boundary, and described today's agentic wave as a deliberate "practice run" — a stress test for society before capabilities become far more powerful. He acknowledged his language was chosen to provoke urgency: "This is partly why I use some of the terms I used, yeah, which were a little bit provocative." Yann LeCun, in a concurrent thread, publicly disagreed, arguing current AI architectures are not on the AGI path — a clean representation of the fork in expert opinion right now.

**Why it matters (PM):** A credible, named CEO with access to frontier models is setting a 3-4 year window in public. Whether or not that timeline is accurate, it is now the planning horizon that boards, governments, and enterprise buyers are internalising. Product strategies that assume a longer runway to transform are being built on an assumption that is no longer the consensus.

**Sources:** [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/deepmind-ceo-demis-hassabis) · [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/deepminds-hassabis-sees-humanity-in-the-foothills-of-the-singularity-while-lecun-says-current-ai-isnt-intelligent/) · [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/20/2026/google-exec-demis-hassabis-predicts-were-at-the-foothills-of-the-singularity)

### Simon Willison: Anthropic and OpenAI have found product-market fit — in enterprise coding agents

In an essay published today, Simon Willison argues April 2026 is the inflection point at which both labs found genuine product-market fit — not in consumer chatbots but in enterprise coding agents. His evidence: Willison's own $200/month subscription covers $2,180+ in token costs — the labs are subsidising power users to drive adoption; Uber maxed its full-year AI budget in a few months, largely via Claude Code; SpaceX's S-1 revealed a $1.25B/month compute commitment from Anthropic; and 26-33% of all open roles at both companies are enterprise sales positions. The shift from "model as product" to "coding agent as product" is, he argues, already operationally underway.

**Why it matters (PM):** The pricing model that underlies this PMF is per-token, not per-seat — and token consumption in coding agents compounds as agents spawn sub-agents. If your team is still evaluating AI spend on per-seat logic, your cost models may be an order of magnitude off for what agentic workflows will actually consume.

**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/27/product-market-fit/)
