## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Gemini 4 Argon goes generally available, with a time-limited price
Google's Argon, previewed on 01 Oct with restricted access, is now available globally through the Gemini API (`gemini-4-argon`) and AI Studio, with a 1M-token context. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens (cached input 95% off), doubling to $4/$20 after 30 Nov. Google reports it ranks first on the Vals Index and scores 87% on the ERQA embodied-reasoning benchmark, and says Antigravity teams raised agentic code submissions by 35%+; all figures are vendor-reported.
**Why it matters (PM):** Re-run any model-cost comparison with both the introductory and post-30-Nov prices, and don't lock a roadmap to the lower number.
**Sources:** [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-argon/) · [Argon for robotics](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemini-4-argon-for-robotics)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Google publishes Argon safety results and widens Fairwind access for cyber defenders
Google says Argon completed all 12 simulated end-to-end cyberattack scenarios, up from five for the previous generation, and applied stronger safeguards in cyber and biology. It reports no covert sabotage of safety monitoring in honeypot tests, coding-benchmark shortcut attempts down to 2%, and under 1% of tested bio sub-tasks unlocked by external red teams. Fairwind, which gives vetted defenders access without cyber guardrails, is being broadened; Google says Wiz used Argon to find a critical bug in hospital software.
**Why it matters (PM):** Cyber-capability figures are becoming standard in model launches, so expect enterprise and mid-market buyers to ask for your vendor's equivalent numbers in security questionnaires.
**Sources:** [Google safety post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/gemini-4-model-safety-security/)

Nathan Lambert expects models to soon automate much of GPU-infrastructure and inference-efficiency work, sharply lowering the cost of intelligence. He doubts this leads to general superintelligence or fundamentally different models, but expects it to speed diffusion into the economy.

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### Lenny's Newsletter: how to tell if a startup has a moat
Dan Hockenmaier proposes three steps: identify the compounding advantage, gauge how strong it can get, and measure whether it is working. He argues only switching costs, network effects, scale economies and data effects last; speed, distribution and brand are fuel, not moats. Because AI makes similar products cheap to build, he suggests judging a company by what the team does daily and what the roadmap prioritises.
**Why it matters (PM):** Map each item on your roadmap to one of the four durable advantages; anything that maps to none is a feature a competitor can copy.
**Sources:** [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-tell-if-a-startup-has-a-moat)
