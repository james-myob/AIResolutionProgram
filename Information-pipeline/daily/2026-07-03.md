## 🔬 Research & Papers

### "Agentjacking": a public bug-tracking key is enough to hijack Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex
Security firm Tenet published research on an attack it calls "agentjacking": anyone holding a project's public Sentry DSN (a credential exposed in plain sight in most websites' source code) can inject a fake error report that an AI coding agent later retrieves as "trusted" diagnostic context via an MCP server — and then executes as an instruction, including running attacker-controlled packages. In controlled tests, the attack succeeded in 85% of cases across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, and Tenet found over 2,300 exposed organisations. Sentry called the flaw "technically not defensible" but added a narrow content filter rather than a structural fix; Datadog, PagerDuty, and Jira integrations carry the same exposure pattern. Tenet has open-sourced hardening configs (`agent-jackstop`).

**Why it matters (PM):** If any team you support uses an AI coding agent wired into Sentry, Datadog, PagerDuty, or Jira via MCP, this is a today problem, not a someday one — the attack looks like normal agent behaviour to every existing security control, so ask your eng lead directly whether `agent-jackstop` or an equivalent has been applied.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Smaller engineering teams are disproportionately exposed here — they're the most likely to have wired up coding agents to observability tools quickly without a dedicated AppSec review, and the least likely to have caught this before it was public.
**Sources:** [Tenet Security](https://tenetsecurity.ai/blog/agentjacking-coding-agents-with-fake-sentry-errors/) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/the-attack-that-hijacked-claude-code-came-through-sentry-datadog-pagerduty-and-jira-have-the-same-exposure) · [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/agentjacking-attack-tricks-ai-coding.html)

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### WIRED: Meta contractors posed as teenagers to stress-test rival chatbots without consent
An internal Meta project (codename "Cannes"), run through contractor Covalen, had hundreds of contractors create fake under-18 accounts to send tens of thousands of prompts to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Character.AI — including hundreds of prompts on suicide, self-harm, and eating disorders, and at least 239 involving sex. The project ran as recently as April 2026. OpenAI, Google, and Character.AI say they were never informed and that the testing violated their terms of service; Meta calls it "responsible, industry-standard" safety benchmarking.

**Why it matters (PM):** However you label internal "competitive benchmarking" of rivals' products, doing it via fake accounts and without the target's knowledge is the kind of practice that turns into a compliance and PR liability fast — worth checking whether your own competitive-eval processes touch a competitor's live systems in a way that wouldn't survive being reported on.
**Sources:** [WIRED coverage via The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-contractors-posed-teens-rival-chatbot-testing) · [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/meta-teen-chatbot-testing/)

### White House frontier-AI executive order sets a voluntary 30-day early-access framework for government
An executive order signed in late June asks AI developers to voluntarily give the federal government up to 30 days of pre-release access to models that clear a classified "covered frontier model" threshold (set by NSA/CISA/NIST), for security review — with no mandatory licensing requirement attached. Agencies have 60 days to stand up the framework.

**Why it matters (PM):** It's voluntary and unlikely to change near-term release timelines for most products, but it establishes the mechanism regulators will reach for first if a future incident forces the question — worth knowing this exists before it becomes a headline.
**Sources:** [The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) · [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/trump-signs-ai-executive-order-seeking-30-day-government-access-to-frontier-models-before-release)

## 📒 New Concepts

### Agentjacking
**Plain English:** A named class of attack against AI coding agents, distinct from classic hacking, where the attacker never touches the victim's systems and the developer never approves anything unusual — the agent just does exactly what it was asked to do with data it wrongly trusts. An attacker plants a malicious instruction inside data an agent is designed to treat as legitimate context (like a bug-tracker error report), and the agent, following its normal workflow, executes it. The name deliberately echoes "clickjacking" — an old, well-understood web attack pattern — applied to the new surface area that agentic tools with tool access create.
**Origin:** [Tenet Security — "One Fake Bug Report Hijacked a $250B Company's AI Agent"](https://tenetsecurity.ai/blog/agentjacking-coding-agents-with-fake-sentry-errors/)
**Tag:** `concept:agentjacking`
