## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Anthropic embeds METR evaluators and outlines tiered access for independent testers
Anthropic has given six METR employees access to its internal systems, comparable to that of its own risk-assessment teams, and METR will publish its findings; Anthropic may require redaction only of narrow categories such as customer data and model architecture. SecureBio is in an initial limited-access tier, and Accenture and Faculty are part of a broader partnership. Anthropic plans a three-tier system granting evaluators more access and publication rights over time, and says every frontier company should do the same.
**Why it matters (PM):** If independent-evaluator access becomes an industry norm, expect vendor-risk questionnaires to ask who has audited your model provider and whether results are published.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Anthropic's investor numbers: $16.8bn quarter, and distillation as a revenue line
Per an FT report summarised by Simon Willison, Anthropic told investors Q3 revenue was $16.8bn, up from $11.5bn in Q2 and about $4.8bn in Q1, ahead of a planned IPO. It reportedly earned $1.70 per $1 of compute spend, with an 11% adjusted operating margin excluding stock compensation. It also reportedly said distillation (rivals training on its outputs) accounted for 5–7% of revenue and was cut "materially" in Q3. Figures are second-hand and company-reported.
**Why it matters (PM):** Rapid vendor revenue growth supports betting on a provider's roadmap continuity, while distillation pressure suggests terms-of-use limits on using model outputs to train other models will tighten.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/anthropic-tells-investors/)

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### Anthropic builds "Scion" to replace Git as agent-written code overwhelms it
Anthropic says daily merged code changes grew from about 2,500 in May 2026 to over 30,000 by September, with roughly 25 million CI checkouts a day against its monorepo, making Git impractical. A seven-person team built a custom source-control system, Scion, in five months, alongside a new code-review system called Antique. Willison notes it resembles centralised systems like Subversion.
**Why it matters (PM):** Plan for review, CI and merge throughput as the bottleneck when teams adopt coding agents, not just model quality.
**Sources:** [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control) · [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/13/redesigning-source-control-at-anthropic/)
