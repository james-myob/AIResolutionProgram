## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Anthropic, NIST's CAISI and Z.ai weigh in on GLM-5.3's cyber capability
Z.ai released GLM-5.3 on 14 August, reporting 84.5% on the CyberGym vulnerability benchmark (vs 83.8% for Claude Mythos 5) and said it would hold open weights back about two weeks for safety review. NIST's CAISI assessed it on 17 September as the most cyber-capable open-weight model to date, but significantly below current U.S. frontier models (roughly four months behind). The Batch's 2 October issue highlights Anthropic's own analysis, which reports the model builds end-to-end exploits almost as often as Claude Mythos Preview and that its safeguards are bypassed by simple tricks 64–100% of the time in simulated tests. Yesterday's briefing carried this only as a Quick Hit; I did not reach Anthropic's report directly, so the Anthropic figures come via secondary summaries.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product lets customers bring or self-host open-weight models, treat built-in model safeguards as absent and put your own guardrails and permissions around anything agentic.
