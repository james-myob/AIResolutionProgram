## 📦 Products & Tooling

### Anthropic launches the Cyber Mission: infrastructure defence programme and a free open-source scanner
Anthropic's Cyber Mission starts with two programmes. The Critical Infrastructure Defense Program gives trusted security providers frontier Claude models, on-site engineers and threat research for operational technology (the systems running grids, water and transport). Founding partners include Accenture, Deloitte, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, PwC and Rockwell Automation. OSS Scanner is an opt-in service giving enrolled open-source projects periodic, free scans with proof-of-concept exploits and suggested fixes. Reports are sent without human review, and Anthropic expects a true-positive rate above 90% (its own figure), so some will contain errors such as wrong severity ratings.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product ships open-source dependencies, expect a rise in model-generated vulnerability reports and budget triage time for them.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Accenture, Deloitte and PwC as founding partners suggest the channel for AI-assisted security work will run through consultancies, which is how many mid-sized firms buy it.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission)

## 💰 Business & Funding

### Anthropic pledges $150M over three years to the US Genesis Mission
Announced at a White House Office of Science and Technology Policy summit, the commitment funds Claude, Claude Code and API credits for several hundred Genesis Mission research projects across more than 15 agencies, including NASA, NIH and NSF. Anthropic also names fusion energy and quantum computing as joint priorities with national labs, and will provide training and onboarding. The post gives no start date or breakdown by agency.
**Why it matters (PM):** Government science programmes are becoming a large, subsidised testbed for agentic coding and research workflows, so features proven there may reach the product roadmap soon.

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### Anthropic's 2026 Usage Policy update takes effect 12 November
Most changes clarify existing rules. Notable additions: a consolidated "deceptive campaigns or artificial activity" section; a rewritten surveillance section barring Claude from deciding who to investigate, arrest or charge; and, for high-risk uses (health, legal rights, finances, livelihood, essential services), a requirement that a qualified human can review and change recommendations and that affected people are told AI was used. Where Claude controls hardware, a qualified operator must be able to observe and stop it, and it must hold a safe state if disconnected. A narrow new ban covers sustained, needless abuse of the model itself.
**Why it matters (PM):** Audit any Claude-powered feature that makes or recommends decisions about people (screening, credit, claims) for a human-override path and AI disclosure before 12 November.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Customers in regulated verticals will start asking vendors how they meet these downstream obligations, so prepare a short answer for sales and procurement.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)
