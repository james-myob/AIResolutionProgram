## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro, open weights promised by 4 Nov
Kimi K3.1 Pro is available now through Moonshot's API at $2 input / $10 output per million tokens, with a "Highspeed" variant that is 2-3x faster at twice the price. Artificial Analysis scores it 55, ahead of Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 4 Argon (53 each) and behind only Claude Opus 5.5 (58); Moonshot's own benchmarks are vendor-reported. Moonshot says full weights will be released by 4 Nov 2026. Note: I could not corroborate the release on Moonshot's own site in this run, so the details rest on Simon Willison's write-up, which links the Kimi announcement.
**Why it matters (PM):** If the weights land as promised, the price and capability gap between open and closed frontier models narrows again, so revisit any build-vs-buy or vendor-lock assumption in your AI roadmap.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Open weights near the frontier make self-hosted or private-cloud deployments more credible for mid-sized customers with data-residency concerns.

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Microsoft turns database-state checks into RL rewards for agents
ThinkingBox-Training uses each task's executable check of the final database state as a pass/fail reward in reinforcement learning. After 50 steps on 187 tasks, Qwen3.8-27B's average success rose from 51.7% to 60.8%, and tasks passed on all 20 attempts rose from 38 to 107. Best-of-20 success dipped slightly (89.4% to 88.0%), so the model became more consistent rather than more capable overall.
**Why it matters (PM):** Reliability on stateful, system-of-record tasks can be improved by training against verifiable outcomes, which suggests an eval-first approach for any agent feature that writes to a database.
**Sources:** [Hugging Face / Microsoft](https://huggingface.co/blog/microsoft/thinkingbox-training)

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Jack Clark tells the IMF the singularity has begun
In Import AI 477, Clark reports on his IMF speech arguing AI is now operating at the frontier of science, sometimes autonomously. He cites Humanity's Last Exam scores rising from about 8% to 67.7% and FrontierMath from 2% to 94%, and urges governments to build energy, compute and lab capacity while keeping strict human oversight of AI-driven research.
**Why it matters (PM):** Expect policy conversations to shift toward capacity and oversight of AI-led research, which can shape procurement and compliance requirements for AI products sold into regulated customers.
**Sources:** [Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-477-in-which-i-tell-the)

### Mollick: 30 months of exponential progress, in charts
Ethan Mollick's data post (tied to his new book *Co-Existence*) says 180 models now beat GPT-4 at its launch, 19 of which run on a phone, and the METR task-length measure rose from about four minutes in 2024 to 17 hours of human-equivalent work by April 2026. He adds a third mode of working with AI, the "conjurer", where people summon agents, delegate and evaluate, and warns it risks losing track of what the AI is doing.
**Why it matters (PM):** The delegate-and-evaluate pattern implies product UX should prioritise review and auditability of agent work, not just prompting.
**Sources:** [One Useful Thing](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/30-months-on-the-exponential-in-charts)
