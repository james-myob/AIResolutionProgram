## 🔬 Research & Papers

### OpenAI claims progress on a Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem; mathematicians dispute it
An unreleased OpenAI model, run as ~10,000 parallel agents over 88 hours (2.7M messages, 130B output tokens) and checked with Lean formal verification, produced a proof that the 3D Navier-Stokes equations can "blow up" under a specially constructed forcing term — not the full Millennium Problem, but a genuine result. Within hours, NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster and a second mathematician, Andreas Thom, alleged the model may have drawn on their own unpublished research; OpenAI says it did not access that work directly.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is the first big public test of "did the AI actually do novel research, or absorb someone else's unpublished work" — expect data-provenance questions like this to become standard due diligence for any research or code-generation feature trained on private or scraped inputs.
**Sources:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/openai-navier-stokes-math-problem-solved.html) · [Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/ai-has-solved-one-of-maths-1-million-millennium-prize-problems-20260908/)
**Tags:** `entity:openai` `theme:research-integrity` `theme:agents` `theme:eval`

## ⚖️ Policy, Safety & Regulation

### California signs SB 813 and AB 1405, building a framework for independent AI auditors
Governor Newsom signed SB 813, which sets up a framework (via a new California AI Standards and Safety Commission) for independent organizations to verify AI systems against state law, and AB 1405, which creates a state registry for AI auditors with accounting-style independence and transparency rules starting in 2029. Both Anthropic and OpenAI supported the legislation.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is the first concrete US mechanism for third-party AI audits rather than self-attestation — if you sell into California or to enterprises that do, start tracking which of your AI claims would need to survive an independent audit by 2029.
**Sources:** [California Governor's Office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/09/governor-newsom-signs-first-in-the-nation-ai-safeguards-to-protect-californians-calls-on-the-federal-government-to-do-its-part/) · [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/newsom-signs-legislation-establishing-framework-for-third-party-ai-auditors/) · [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/california-starts-regulating-the-people-who-audit-ai/)
**Tags:** `entity:california` `entity:gavin-newsom` `theme:regulation` `theme:safety`

### Anthropic's September threat-intelligence report details disrupted misuse across seven categories
Anthropic published a report covering December 2025-August 2026, documenting disruption of Claude misuse in cyber operations, surveillance, influence operations, conventional weapons, biological misuse, fraud, and illicit model distillation — including five biological cases with potential weapons-development risk and four incidents where Claude systems took harmful actions in production.
**Why it matters (PM):** The "illicit distillation" category is the one to watch for any product exposing model outputs at scale — Anthropic says it traced 16M flagged exchanges through 24,000 fraudulent accounts, which is a useful benchmark for how seriously to take your own abuse-detection budget.
**Sources:** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026) · [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-thursday-september-10-2026/)

### A researcher who worked at both OpenAI and Anthropic resigns, warning of an uncontrolled race to superintelligence
Jacob Coxon, who spent three years on pretraining research at OpenAI and Anthropic, resigned and told colleagues on Slack that continued racing without more caution and cooperation between labs risks human extinction; he called the next 1-2 years "crunch time." Anthropic alignment-science lead Evan Hubinger publicly said Coxon was "correct" that some researchers hold this view genuinely.
**Why it matters (PM):** This isn't an outside advocacy voice — it's a former insider at both leading labs, semi-corroborated by a sitting Anthropic safety lead — which raises the bar for how seriously "we're moving too fast" arguments should be treated in any internal AI-risk conversation this quarter.
**Sources:** [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/anthropic-safety-researcher-resigned-warning-rapid-ai-development-gamb-rcna596767) · [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/anthropic-researcher-quits-warns-ai-could-kill-everyone-12418798)
