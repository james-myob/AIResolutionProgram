## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Anthropic ships Claude Opus 5.5, its first model since calling for an industry slowdown
Opus 5.5 matches Claude Fable 5.1 on most work and beats it on agentic coding, computer use, and multidisciplinary reasoning, while costing 40% less to run ($4/$20 per million input/output tokens) and generating output over 30% faster. It's available now on Anthropic's own platform plus AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure, with Sonnet and Haiku 5.5 variants coming.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is the same lab that publicly called for pacing frontier development, shipping a model that's both more capable and cheaper than its predecessor within weeks — treat "pacing" statements as a safety position, not a signal that price/performance gains are slowing down.

### OpenAI cuts API prices 50% with GPT-6 Sol and Luna
GPT-6 Sol lands at $2/$10 per million input/output tokens (down from GPT-5.6 Sol's $4/$20) and GPT-6 Luna at $0.10/$0.50 — permanent pricing, not a promotion, an OpenAI spokesperson confirmed. OpenAI credits inference and caching improvements for cutting cost while adding capability, rounding out a three-tier family from Astra ($10/$50) down to Luna.
**Why it matters (PM):** A second frontier lab halving prices in the same week resets the per-token cost baseline product teams plan against — any AI feature cost model built even a month ago is worth re-running against current pricing before your next planning cycle.

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Claude's biology lab surfaces a previously unknown, CRISPR-like enzyme system
Anthropic's biology lab — open only since this spring — says Claude identified a novel enzyme system in bacteriophage DNA capable of cutting, copying, and pasting genetic material, similar in principle to CRISPR. Around 950 Claude-run agents spent ~21 hours and 210M tokens on the analysis; Dario Amodei was careful to note the finding builds on prior Stanford work on a similar system, and human scientists ran all physical lab work.
**Why it matters (PM):** This is a concrete "AI accelerates the research funnel, humans still do the wet-lab verification" case study — useful as a template for scoping AI-augmented R&D workflows in any domain where a wrong answer is expensive to unwind.

## 📈 Industry Analysis

### Stratechery: labs' "pace the frontier" safety framing looks like a response to business overhangs
Ben Thompson argues Anthropic and OpenAI face five "overhangs" — capability (adequate harnesses now compete without frontier models), product, pricing (compute scarcity, not real cost advantage, drives high prices), capital (revenue must grow fast before funding tightens), and safety (real but worsened, not eased, by slower pacing). His read: pacing calls conveniently solve frontier labs' commercial problems while being framed as safety principle.
**Why it matters (PM):** A useful skeptic's checklist for the next lab announcement framed around "responsible pacing" — ask which of these five business pressures it might actually be solving before taking the safety framing at face value.
**Sources:** [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/frontier-overhangs/)
**Tags:** `entity:anthropic` `entity:openai` `theme:safety` `theme:pricing`

## 🛠️ Product Practice

### OpenAI fires contractors for using AI tools to rate ChatGPT's own answers
Contractors on "Project Lily," hired to read real ChatGPT conversations and rate response quality (including catching sycophancy and anthropomorphizing), were terminated for using LLMs, GPTZero, Grammarly, and AI translation tools on the job — against explicit guidelines. OpenAI uses human review specifically to avoid the model learning from its own synthetic judgments.
**Why it matters (PM):** If your product roadmap includes a "humans rate AI outputs to train/eval the next version" step, this is a live example of that pipeline quietly contaminating itself — worth auditing whether your own eval or RLHF contractors have the same loophole.
**Sources:** [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/people-training-openais-ai-fired-for-using-ai-to-train-the-ai/) · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/openai-fires-contractors/)
