## 🔬 Research & Papers

### NVIDIA Kumo Relational: a pre-trained model for predictions over relational databases
NVIDIA describes Kumo Relational as a foundation model that predicts outcomes such as churn, demand or fraud from enterprise databases in one forward pass, using up to 10,000 historical examples as in-context data. It needs no feature engineering or table flattening. Across 41 tasks on 15 databases, NVIDIA reports about 4 AUROC points over XGBoost on RelBench V1 classification and an 8% mean-reciprocal-rank gain over AutoGluon ensembles on SAP SALT (16% after fine-tuning). These are NVIDIA's own results.
**Why it matters (PM):** If a general model matches hand-built pipelines on tabular business data, prediction features become cheaper to scope and ship, and the moat shifts to having clean relational data.
**Why it matters (mid-market):** Mid-sized vendors rarely have data-science teams; a no-training prediction layer over customers' existing databases is a plausible packaging route for churn or fraud scoring.
**Sources:** [Hugging Face / NVIDIA](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/kumo-relational)

### VocabX: specialising a small model's vocabulary cut inference cost 77% in one case
VocabX rebuilds a small model's tokenizer around per-token cost, merging repeated domain spans into single "macro tokens" and pruning unused entries, with the base model and training recipe unchanged. On a Portuguese essay grader built on Qwen3-4B, measured inference cost fell 77.0%, average responses shrank from 350 to 101 tokens and the bench score rose from 0.703 to 0.810. The authors say it beat the strongest reference method in all 15 domain-and-model combinations, but the paper is under review and the cost figure comes from one hardware and workload setup.
**Why it matters (PM):** For high-volume, narrow tasks, tokenizer-level tuning is a cost lever that sits alongside model choice and distillation; treat the 77% as an upper bound until reproduced.
**Sources:** [Hugging Face / Dharma-AI](https://huggingface.co/blog/Dharma-AI/vocabx-vocabulary-specialization)

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### IBM ships Granite Speech 5.0 under Apache 2.0
The family includes a 2B-parameter model, a 0.5B "turbo CTC" model and an updated 4.1 2B-plus for multi-speaker audio. IBM reports 5.06% average English word error rate for the 2B model, second among top open-weight ASR models, with transcription and translation in six languages and keyword lists of up to 2,000 terms. Turbo CTC reaches 19,870 RTFx (audio processed per unit of compute time) on the Open ASR Leaderboard, about 1.6× its launch speed.
**Why it matters (PM):** Keyword biasing for product names and jargon, plus a permissive licence, makes self-hosted transcription a credible alternative to per-minute API pricing.
**Sources:** [Hugging Face / IBM Granite](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-speech-5)

### Wiz: AI tools helped find a PHP RCE; two PHP 8.x flaws patched early after leaks
Simon Willison relays that two remote-code-execution flaws in PHP 8.x were patched on 21 October after leaked CVE IDs and a mistakenly published proof-of-concept video let exploits circulate early. Wiz Research, which found the first bug, says it used AI tools including Wiz Atlas, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol to discover it (Wiz's account).
**Why it matters (PM):** If AI-assisted discovery is becoming routine, expect more disclosures and shorter patch windows; check that your PHP-based dependencies and customers' stacks can take emergency updates.
**Sources:** [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/21/php-security-release/)
