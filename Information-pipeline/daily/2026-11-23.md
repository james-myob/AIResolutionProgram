## 🧠 Models & Capabilities

### Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini, a "looped" open-weights model
The 26.4B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (a design that activates only part of the network per token) repeats its 12 middle layers three times per token, which keeps it to about 20 GB on disk. The model card recommends at least 32 GB of memory on Apple Silicon. Thinking Machines reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1; independent evaluator Artificial Analysis says it matched Qwen3.8-27B on its Intelligence Index with about five times fewer active parameters, at $0.59 versus $3.53 per benchmark task. The company acknowledges weaker results on complex instructions and hard reasoning; weights are Apache 2.0.
**Why it matters (PM):** If you have ruled out on-device or self-hosted models on cost or memory grounds, re-run that estimate against this class of model before your next roadmap review.
**Sources:** [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/thinking-machines-inkling-2-mini-looped-ai-memory/)

## 📦 Products & Tooling

### OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo, a live-patching "shield" runtime for Linux
Shirudo uses eBPF (a Linux feature for running small, sandboxed programs inside the kernel) to load temporary "shields" that block the code path an exploit uses in a running system, with no restart. In a controlled test, the companies say a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell attempt against a running Elasticsearch process. Some features, such as signed shields and TPM-backed records, are described as goals still being designed. Code is BSD 2-Clause.
**Why it matters (PM):** AI-generated mitigations applied in minutes rather than a patch cycle are becoming a reference point for security features, so expect customers to ask what your product does when a zero-day lands.
**Sources:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/)

## 🔬 Research & Papers

### Architect Labs says AI agents designed "Frostbite", a CPU claimed 4.59x more power-efficient
Announced 18 Nov, the design uses interaction nets and a new instruction set, and the company says agents tested 58 ideas and kept 37. The 4.59x performance-per-watt figure is a geometric mean over 25 workloads in pre-silicon simulation, against an unnamed comparison core that Tom's Hardware, cited by The Neuron, identifies as dual-issue and in-order. Nothing has been fabricated; treat it as a company claim.
**Why it matters (PM):** Agent-driven design loops are reaching hardware; the claim is unproven, but it signals where agent capability is being pushed beyond software.
**Sources:** [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/ai-designed-frostbite-cpu-architect-labs/)
